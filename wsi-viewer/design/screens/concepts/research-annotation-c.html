Design rationale · concept C "Task-flow" · what to look at, trade-offs, engine contracts

What this concept does differently

The workspace is organised around the sequence a reviewer actually follows, Open → Screen → Examine → Annotate → Export, instead of around panels. The tool rail (region C) becomes a step rail; the context dock (region D) becomes one step's panel at a time, with the current step's tools nested under it. Everything a step needs is in reach and nothing else is on screen, which is what a learner, a consultant on an iPad or a frozen-section reviewer wants. Power users get two escape hatches that cost nothing when unused: the "all steps" toggle stacks every panel (the classic dock), and the command palette (Ctrl K) reaches every command from any step. The same five steps are the bottom bar on tablets and phones, so the flow scales down without a second information architecture.

Key choices

  1. Progressive disclosure by step, not by panel. The dock shows one step; its eyebrow says "Step 4 of 5 · Annotate", its title says what to do, its footer carries the single primary action ("Next: Export"). The step rail shows done / current / next with luminance and a check glyph, never colour.
  2. Tools live under their step. Annotation tools appear only in Annotate (and in the pen-tip palette), compare and channel controls only in Examine. The keymap is global, so P still draws a polygon from any step; the step changes what is visible, not what is possible.
  3. Mobile-first geometry. Below 1080 px the step panel is an in-flow peeking sheet: the canvas yields height rather than being covered, so P1's "nothing over the centre" holds even when the sheet is expanded. The tray under the canvas holds the step's tools at 44 px; the bottom bar holds the five steps.
  4. AI outline first, heatmap on demand; threshold and opacity are uniform updates; every output row carries the provenance tuple and the research status tag; in the Dx build the fixed not-validated marker appears on the layer.
  5. Honest pixels. The sharpness indicator in the strip (and in the HUD on phones) reads Sharp / Sharpening… / Soft — reason / Offline; the demo strip reproduces cold 4G, offline, tile errors, translator slow, "level not yet available", Tier 2, GPU reset and the two plates. The canvas is never blank: failed tiles keep the ancestor.
  6. Product and mode unmistakable. The RUO badge and the full string sit first in the top bar at every width ≥ 1024; below that the string is one tap away behind the badge and in About. The Clinical-IH / Dx variants add the NAV / DX indicator, the session-purpose chip, the accession and the label image placeholder; the RUO build refuses a clinical launch with the fixed plate.
  7. CVD-safe palette with an author-side check. QuPath class names are verbatim; on-screen colours are the design system's luminance-separated palette; the deuteranopia / protanopia simulation runs on the canvas and the swatches so a reviewer sees what a dichromat sees before encoding meaning in colour.

Trade-offs and the risk the brief names

  • Friction for power users is the stated risk. Mitigations: all-steps mode; the palette; global keys (N / ⇧N step, \ panel); the step rail is 88 px, wider than a tool rail, which buys ≈ 2 % of canvas width on a 1920 display. A pathologist who annotates 200 slides a day will live in all-steps mode and the palette; the step rail then degrades to a progress indicator, which is still useful.
  • Steps imply an order the work does not always have. Examine and Annotate interleave constantly. The steps are therefore not gates: any step is one click away, nothing is locked, and the only sequencing that matters (export after annotation) is encoded as the footer's primary action, not as a wall.
  • Laptop (1080–1599 px) follows the design system: the panel is a drawer over the canvas edge, closed by default to keep P1's ≥ 70 % canvas; the current step opens it. The judges should look at both 1440 × 900 screenshots (closed and open).
  • Multiplex channels are a section inside Examine, which is one level deeper than a dedicated panel. For spatial biologists this is a real cost; the channel grid view and the palette commands reduce it, and all-steps mode removes it.

What the judges should look at

  • Switch steps with the demo control or N and watch the rail tools, the panel, the footer action and the canvas aria-label change together: one registry drives all four.
  • Zoom with + / −, the wheel (pointer-anchored) or 1–6: the magnification readout, the µm/px line, the 1/2/5 scale bar, the navigator rectangle and the detection LOD (centroids below 10×, outlines above) update without the chrome moving.
  • Set the state to Cold 4G and zoom: Sharpening… turns into Soft — slow link; set Tile errors: three tiles show the ancestor with a dashed edge and the strip says so.
  • Set the build to Clinical-IH or Dx: badge, string, NAV / DX, purpose chip, label image, QA chip and the not-validated marker appear; set the state to Launch refused: the fixed plate, nothing opened.
  • Resize to 1024 × 1366 or 390 × 844 (or open the screenshots): steps become the bottom bar, tools the tray, the panel a peeking sheet; on the phone the drawing tools reduce to point and measure (Design note D9) and the sharpness dot joins the HUD.
  • Toggle Live markers: every element the engine drives is outlined with its contract; Centre guide: nothing in the DOM intersects the central 50 %.

Accessibility notes

  • Canvas is role="application" with a descriptive label and aria-describedby → polite live region; HUD values are announced at settle, debounced 500 ms.
  • Every drag has a single-pointer alternative: navigator pan buttons, Fly there, click-to-place vertices, two-click measurement, curtain slider, typeable sliders (SC 2.5.7). Targets are 24 px, 44 px on coarse pointers (SC 2.5.8).
  • Annotations, candidates, layers and slides are real lists (listbox/option, Tab moves, Space flies). The class picker is a radiogroup with keys 1–8. The keymap and About replace the panel, never the slide.
  • No meaning by colour alone: state tags, dots and glyphs carry text; swatches carry pattern text in legends; the CVD simulation is one click away.
  • Reduced motion: the fly-to tween is skipped; chrome transitions are zeroed by the design system; nothing animates over the canvas during a gesture.

Design notes (departures and gaps the plan owner should see)

  • C-1 Region C is a step rail, not a tool rail; tools nest under the current step. The shared viewer core still has the full tool set (keymap, palette, pen-tip palette), so the pillar adds a panel arrangement, not canvas behaviour (plan 12 §1 corollary).
  • C-2 New bindings N / ⇧N (step) and \ (panel) use keys free in maps A and B; N is also Learn's "next step", so the mental model is shared.
  • C-3 In the Clinical-IH variant the session purpose shown is QA review · blinded: an in-house model review is a QA purpose under D1's four fixed strings; if counsel wants a fifth string, it is one token.
  • C-4 The HPF grid and the tile-error patches are DOM/SVG placeholders here; in the product they are GPU HUD layers (Design note D11).
  • C-5 Threshold and opacity ranges are plain <input type="range">; the product binds them to one-frame uniform updates (US-R2).

Known weaknesses (honest)

  • The step metaphor costs one click for anyone who switches between Examine and Annotate constantly; all-steps mode fixes it but then the concept is close to a conventional dock with a progress rail.
  • Multiplex controls are a level deeper than a spatial biologist would want; the channel grid and palette help, a dedicated step would be the alternative.
  • The placeholder canvas is an SVG pattern; at 20–40× it looks like a pattern, not tissue, and the tissue patterns are not colour-managed. The zoom demo exists to show the HUD and LOD language, not pixels.
  • Pinch, rotate and drag-with-pen are not simulated; the pen variant is a static illustration of "pen draws, finger pans".
  • Accept/reject write-back, the uncertainty queue and the local tiler are shown as states, not behaviour; the export jobs are static rows.
