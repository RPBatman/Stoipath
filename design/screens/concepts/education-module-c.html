        Design rationale · Concept C · Task-flow · what the judges should look at

        Concept C of three for the Education module. Revision 2026-11-01 of the 2026-10-26 draft. The plan (`docs/plan/12` §5, `02` FR-E1–E12, NFR-A1–A5) and `design/principles-and-ia.md` are the sources of truth; departures are listed as design notes at the end.

        1. What this concept does differently

        • The shell is the task sequence. Region C is a step rail (Open → Screen → Examine → Decide → Complete) instead of a tool rail; region D is a single task card with exactly one primary action, 56 px tall, at its foot. Tools appear under the phase that needs them (progressive disclosure); an All tools switch and the command palette restore everything for power users.
        • One flow, four builds. The same five phases carry the learner module (9 steps; 7 for the resident unknown), the faculty authoring studio (Collect → Record → Edit → Items → Publish), the Clinical-IH consult on an iPad or a frozen section (Open → Screen → Examine → Decide → Hand off) and the RUO workspace (… → Annotate → Export). The build decides what the last step is allowed to be: Learn submits to a course, Clinical-IH hands off to an attested display, Viewer exports GeoJSON, nothing ever signs out from here.
        • Tablet first, scaling up. At 1024 × 1366 the rail becomes a bottom flow bar and the card a bottom sheet that peeks (title + primary action, thumb-reachable) and expands on demand; at 1440 × 900 the card is a drawer closed by default and a one-line task bar keeps the step and its action visible while the canvas gets 87 %; at 1920 and 4K the card docks. Pen draws, finger pans; every pen action has a finger and a keyboard equivalent.
        • The keyboard path is the same path. N/⇧N step, ↵ runs the card's primary action, ⇧↵ opens Mark without a pointer (candidates A–F with Fly there, which also meets the zoom gate; or a structured text answer), ⇧R reveals. The author cannot publish a region item without one of those alternatives (NFR-A5 is a publish blocker, not a guideline).
        • Reveal is a gate, not a button. The answer key is a layer with ACL revealOn(itemAnswered); the card shows Answer saved · ⇧R to reveal after commit and the key only after the event. Confidence feeds the spaced-retrieval queue (step 8), never the grade.
        • Honest pixels in the flow. The zoom gate (Zoom to at least 20× to earn full credit) reports the highest magnification reached over the focus, the strip reports Sharp / Sharpening… / Soft — slow link / Offline — cached tiles only with the §5 strings, and the item engine will not reveal while a tile under the mark is stale (plan 01: "a region-marking item with a soft tile is an unfair item").

        2. Trade-offs and known weaknesses

        • Friction for power users (the angle's stated risk). A linear flow slows a resident who wants to free-roam. Mitigations: the step rail is also navigation (any reached phase is one tap away), Skip to the question, the All tools switch, the palette, and the resident flow drops narration and has 7 steps. Not solved: a faculty member reviewing many cases will want a list view, which is the collection browser (a separate screen).
        • Region C changes role. The IA names region C the tool rail; here it is a step rail that contains the phase's tools. Width grows from 56 to 84 px on desktop (64 px on laptop/tablet landscape) to fit a number and a label; the canvas share at rest is ≈ 71 % at 1920 × 1080 standalone and ≈ 70 % inside the LMS iframe with the card docked (≥ 70 % passes P1, but with no margin to spare; a tool-rail concept keeps ≈ 2 points more).
        • The expanded bottom sheet intrudes on the central zone. The peek (196 px / 176 px on phones) stays below the central 50 % × 50 %; the expanded sheet (≤ 40 % of viewport) does not. It is user-invoked, transient and dismissible, and the HUD corners rise above it, but it is a P1 exception the final design must either accept or replace with a side sheet in landscape.
        • One primary action means secondary actions are a step down. Previous, Skip and All keys sit in a smaller row under the CTA; on phones they wrap. The cost is deliberate; the benefit is that a learner on a 4G phone never hunts for "what now".
        • The simulated canvas. The tissue is an inline SVG pattern; sharpness, gate, scoring, fly-to and the heatmap are mock behaviours marked data-live. Toggle Live markers in the demo strip to see every element the engine or an integration would drive.
        • Clinical and RUO variants are proof of scaling, not the owning screens. The clinical sign-out and research concepts own those workflows; here they show that the flow shell does not fork per build.

        5. Design notes (conflicts flagged for the plan owner)

        • DN-C1 · Region C. The IA defines region C as the tool rail. This concept widens it to a step rail that discloses tools per phase. If the final design adopts it, principles §3.1 should name the region "step/tool rail" and the 84 px width should be confirmed against P1 at 1920 × 1080 (≈ 70–71 % here).
        • DN-C2 · ⇧E "Expert's view". A Learn addition not in the IA keymap (§4.5 lists N, ⇧N, Space, ⇧↵, ⇧R for Learn). Free in map A/B; proposed for the registry.
        • DN-C3 · Tier chip in Learn. Plan 02 §5.1 shows the tier "whenever it limits a mode"; Learn has no Dx, so Tier 2 limits nothing, yet the screen brief (§10.3) requires Tier 2 · 60 Hz on the iPad variant. Shown here as information; its sheet says "Nothing in this module needs Tier 1".
        • DN-C4 · "Submit to your course" versus AGS. FR-E4 posts each item's score automatically on itemAnswered. The final step is therefore a completion marker (xAPI completed, course progress), not a grade gate; the CTA wording should not imply that grades wait for it.
        • DN-C5 · Expanded sheet and P1. See §2; owner to decide between accepting the transient exception and a landscape side sheet.
        • DN-C6 · Audience-scoped step counts. The IA stepper example is "Step 4 of 9"; the resident unknown uses 7 steps (no narration, diagnosis before reveal). The module schema should allow per-audience step lists over one case.
