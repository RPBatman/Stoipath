## Summary

1. **Pathology EPAs are real, published and voluntary.** The College of American Pathologists GME Committee proposed 19 EPAs in 2017 as "an initial framework" (McCloskey et al., Acad Pathol 2017, PMID 28725792 **[PM, PM-full]**). An APC/CAP national pilot tested four of them in 2021–22 and found them feasible, with faculty less favourable than programme directors and 28 % (5 of 18) of CCC members saying EPA summaries did not help them assign Milestones (Bryant et al., Acad Pathol 2024, PMID 38560425 **[PM, PM-full]**). No retrieved source shows ACGME or ABPath requiring EPAs in pathology **[unverified: absence of evidence]**. What is adopted is the ACGME Pathology Milestones (27 in version 1, 21 in version 2) reviewed semi-annually by a Clinical Competency Committee (Klutts et al., Acad Pathol 2015, PMID 28725752 **[PM, PM-full]**; Poljak et al., Acad Pathol 2025, PMID 41541631 **[PM]**).
2. **There is no sign-out EPA as such.** Sign-out sits inside "Compose a diagnostic report for surgical pathology specimens" (EPA 2 in the paper's table order) and the cytology report EPA. The paper names the observation problem a simulation can answer: attendings "often rely upon a correct diagnosis as a marker for adequate evaluation of a case", and observing the microscopy process "may provide insight into inefficient work habits" that hinder the transition to independent practice (PMID 28725792 **[PM-full]**).
3. **Entrustment is a supervisor's judgement about real work.** Simulation sits at Miller's "shows how"; ten Cate and colleagues propose a "trusted" level above "does" (Acad Med 2021, PMID 33060399 **[PM]**). In the only retrieved study comparing simulation-based EPA ratings with clinical ones, raters agreed well on the simulation tool (ICC 0.65–0.77) but the two sets of ratings did not correlate (r = −0.35 and 0.38; 28 residents; Andler et al., J Grad Med Educ 2020, PMID 32322347 **[PM]**). A simulated sign-out can therefore supply a standardised data point that informs an entrustment decision; it cannot make one.
4. **The gap it fills is real.** In a survey of 142 programme directors (32 analysed, 22.5 %), no programme let residents sign out and issue a final report under ACGME "oversight supervision", which the paper describes as review and feedback after care is delivered and as recently added to the core requirements (Bryant et al., Acad Pathol 2025, PMID 41049715 **[PM, PM-full]**; the ACGME text itself was not read **[unverified]**). A five-year landscape found most programmes did not measure competency before granting autonomy (Miller et al., Acad Pathol 2024, PMID 39381326 **[PM]**).
5. **The nearest deployed analogue is radiology's simulated independent call** (WIDI SIM: 65 cases per 8-hour session, free-text answers graded by faculty on a 0–10 rubric, 992 sessions from 47 programmes over five cycles; case difficulty explained 28.5 % of score variance against 5.3 % for the resident and 0.58 % for the grader; Sistrom et al., Acad Radiol 2020, PMIDs 32376185 and 32402787 **[PM]**). Even this mature system grades free text by hand, and its programme site says the 2025 cycle is the last **[snippet]**. In pathology the precedents are small and single-site: the OSPE (Belovarac et al., Am J Clin Pathol 2021, PMID 33049036 **[PM]**), Toronto's Hot Seat Diagnosis (Han et al., Arch Pathol Lab Med 2022, PMID 34133708 **[PM]**), Iowa's preliminary-diagnosis dual review (Weydert et al., Am J Surg Pathol 2005, PMID 15897747 **[PM]**) and a Louisville preliminary-versus-final feedback system (Cao and Gondim, Arch Pathol Lab Med 2026, doi:10.5858/arpa.2025-0421-OA, verified in note 11-gap-6; not re-retrieved here **[PM-note]**). ABPath has announced a voluntary, formative simulated sign-out pilot in breast pathology **[snippet only]**.
6. **Automated grading is defensible for structured content and not yet validated for diagnostic reports.** Large-language-model scoring of short free-text answers reaches near-human agreement in single-institution studies (902 responses: human–human QWK 0.90–0.92, best models 0.88–0.90, weaker models below 0.63; Sidali et al., JMIR Med Educ 2026, PMID 42970784 **[PM]**), but the authors say agreement is not validity and call for multicentre validation and drift safeguards. No retrieved study validates automated grading of a trainee's pathology or radiology report **[unverified: absence of evidence]**. No body has adopted automated entrustment; the entrustment-and-AI framework in Academic Medicine applies entrustment to the AI tool, not to the trainee (Gin et al., Acad Med 2025, PMID 39761533 **[PM]**).
7. **Viewing behaviour predicts accuracy at group level, not for individuals.** Zoom/pan features classified accurate versus inaccurate diagnoses with AUC 0.86 in 140 pathologists (Brunyé et al., JAMIA 2024, PMID 38031453 **[PM]**); in 155 residents each training year gave 19 % higher odds of an accurate annotation and 18 % of an accurate diagnosis, with accurate feature annotation the key predictor in an exploratory analysis (Brunyé et al., Mod Pathol 2025, PMID 40441531 **[PM]**); an eye-tracking study found no single viewing behaviour associated with accuracy (Brunyé et al., J Med Imaging 2020, PMID 37476351 **[PM]**). Viewing-trace metrics are formative feedback and research data, not a competence score.
8. **Spaced and adaptive practice have the strongest learning evidence** (RCT, 724 urology residents, histopathology images: long-term gain +15.2 % vs +3.4 %; Kerfoot et al., J Am Coll Surg 2010, PMID 20800189 **[PM]**; meta-analysis SMD 0.78 across 21,415 learners, mostly text and flashcard interventions; Maye and Hurley, Clin Teach 2026, PMID 41601436 **[PM]**). Adaptive histopathology modules show large pre/post gains but are non-randomised, partly developer-run, and several lack retention tests (Krasne 2013, PMID 24524000; Parker 2017, PMID 28725787; Knapp 2022, PMID 36268109 **[PM]**).
9. **Reliability needs many observations and the rater is often the largest error term.** Workplace entrustment studies needed 9–11 observations per EPA for 0.7 (Dunne et al., PMID 34561828 **[PM]**), 25–63 where rater variance was 30–50 % and student variance 3.5–8 % (Ryan et al., PMID 33464735 **[PM]**), and reached only 0.40 for a typical month in a residency (Kelleher et al., PMID 31567170 **[PM]**). Reference-based automatic scoring removes the rater term for the objective part; the case count a simulated sign-out needs is a design target to measure, not assume.
10. **What the current Stoipath build is, honestly:** a formative practice report on one task (axillary sentinel-node staging, 10 CAMELYON17 cases) with an irreversible submission, no reference before submission, discrepancy classes with direction, a viewing-trace miss type, versioned exports and a use statement **[repo]**. It has no escalation or confidence capture, no free-text scoring, no reviewer, no entrustment scale, no case breadth, no reliability or extrapolation evidence and no integrity on its exports. Plan section 14 designs the additions.

### 1.2 Burden and acceptance

- Toronto anatomical pathology (EPAs since 2019): 25 of 30 residents responded; 92 % disagreed that EPAs gave valuable feedback; all 25 agreed verbal feedback at sign-out was among the most useful assessments; 76 % spent at least half an hour a week on EPA paperwork; respondents felt EPAs hurt staff–resident rapport (Xie et al., Arch Pathol Lab Med 2026;150(6):465-470, PMID 41571013 **[PM]**).
- A multi-specialty review of early EPA studies: feasible; barriers are faculty engagement, workflow friction, sampling bias and redundant systems (J Surg Educ 2026;83(9):104082, PMID 42492437 **[PM-note]**).
- Fellows entering practice named high case load, signing out outside their subspecialty and time management as their first-year challenges (12 fellows, 4 institutions; Katsakhyan et al., Arch Pathol Lab Med 2024, PMID 38180066 **[PM]**).

### 1.3 How CCCs use data

- Iowa, Milestones 1.0: each resident reviewed on every milestone at least semi-annually; inputs were rotation evaluations, prior ratings, self-ratings, in-service examination scores, portfolios and 360° evaluations; members drafted ratings for a few milestones each and agreed in about 90-minute meetings (PMID 28725752 **[PM-full]**).
- Internal medicine, one site: CCC members weighted rotation ratings 37 %, faculty comments 27 %, personal experience 14 % (Ekpenyong et al., Med Teach 2017, PMID 28738746 **[PM]**).
- Otolaryngology focus groups: "narrative and verbal feedback outweighed structured data"; data collected and unused; focus on finding struggling residents (Erickson et al., Laryngoscope 2026, PMID 41431334 **[PM]**).
- In the pathology pilot, CCC members reported more information and confidence, yet 28 % said EPA summaries did not help at all (PMID 38560425 **[PM-full]**).

Implication: a CCC needs a short, narrative-bearing, trustworthy summary per resident; raw numeric exports compete with a flood of other inputs.

### 1.4 Milestone subcompetencies a simulated sign-out can inform

Version 2 names retrieved from a 2025 paper (PC1 Reporting, PC2 Grossing, PC4 Interpretation and Diagnosis, MK2 Clinical Reasoning, SBP1, SBP2, PBLI2 Reflective Practice and Commitment to Personal Growth, ICS3 Communication with Health Care Systems, PROF3 Self-awareness and help-seeking; PMID 41541631 **[PM-full]**). Codes must be confirmed against the current ACGME PDF before any export column is named after one. Mapping (author inference): PC4 is what the current tool measures; PC1 only if a free-text report line is scored; MK2 and PBLI2 only if reasoning or reflection is captured; PROF3 only if confidence and escalation are captured; ICS3 only with a communication station; SBP1 only with quality traps. The CAP paper places EPA assessment at Miller's "does"; a simulation is "shows how".

## 2. Simulated independent practice: precedents and mechanics

### 2.1 Pathology

| Programme | What it is | What it measured | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| OSPE (NYU) | OSCE adapted for pathology: an AP sign-out station (diagnosis and the key information for cancer staging reports), a simulated frozen section with communication to a role-played "surgeon", and quality-trap cases (mismatched slides, gross-description irregularities); every case scored against the ACGME Milestones | Feasibility and tracking; no reliability statistics in the abstract | Belovarac et al., Am J Clin Pathol 2021, PMID 33049036 **[PM]** |

### 2.2 Radiology (the strongest analogue)

- **WIDI SIM** (University of Florida): full-resolution clinical images in clinical-grade software, 65 cases per 8-hour session including normals, five yearly cycles 2014–2018, 992 sessions, residents from 47 programmes; free-text answers graded 0–10 by faculty after the fact; 27.0 % of 63,839 scores fell in the 0–2 "clinically significant miss" band; neurological cases 2.3 points harder; a small significant decline over the last 13 cases (fatigue); training effect 2.2 points from mid-R1 to late-R3 (Part 1, PMID 32376185 **[PM]**). Variance components: case 28.5 %, resident 5.3 %, programme 1.1 %, grader 0.58 % (Part 2, PMID 32402787 **[PM]**). Rubric bands 0–2 critical error, 3–6 problematic omissions, 7–10 effective, with errors typed observational versus interpretive (Amador et al., Curr Probl Diagn Radiol 2025, PMID 39904678 **[PM-note]**). A later analysis holds 8,488 scores from 52 programmes on 35 paediatric trauma cases (Pierre et al., Emerg Radiol 2024, PMID 39549206 **[PM]**). The programme site gives an 8.5-hour shift, an EPA link and that the 2025 cycle is the last **[snippet]**; the abstracts say "8 hour" and never "overnight". Cases given with a leading history were associated with a higher correct-call rate across four cycles, but history was not randomised and cases differed by year, so this is observational and confounded with case (Sharma et al., Pediatr Radiol 2021, PMID 33442781 **[PM]**).
- **Earlier DICOM call simulations** at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center / Harvard: a first pilot found no level difference (Ganguli et al., Acad Radiol 2006, PMID 16679280 **[PM-note]**); over five years PGY-2 residents scored 71 % against 79–86 % for PGY-3 to PGY-5 (Ganguli et al., AJR 2009, PMID 19155422 **[PM-note]**). "UPMC" in an earlier draft was an error.
- **Transfer to practice:** 12 UK trainees with simulation on-call training versus 12 historical controls, 2,320 first on-call cases: total discrepancies fell (p = 0.01), major discrepancies improved but not significantly (Butt et al., Clin Radiol 2023, PMID 36804272 **[PM]**). This is the only outcome-on-real-practice result retrieved for a diagnostic simulation.
- **Discrepancy grading schemes:** RADPEER-style score-based peer review was criticised as punitive and a poor evaluator of performance, motivating peer learning (Parrott et al., Curr Probl Diagn Radiol 2023, PMID 37069021 **[PM]**); current ACR RADPEER definitions **[snippet, unverified]**. Real preliminary-report discrepancy rates differ by study and definition (abdominal CT/US 5.6 % any, 2.6 % important, PMID 36084145; neuroradiology emergency MRI 16 % level 2+3, 5.5 % major, PMID 35435441; echocardiography 16.8 %, 2.4 % major, PMID 28870437; all **[PM-note]**), which is itself a lesson for defining "major".

### 2.3 Error taxonomies and guidelines usable for grading

- Meier et al.'s amended-report taxonomy (misinterpretation, misidentification, specimen defects, report defects) was validated on 430 cases (kappa 0.878; 0.62 across 7 institutions); amendment rates 2.6–4.8 per 1,000 reports (Am J Clin Pathol 2008, PMID 18628093 **[PM]**). Directly reusable for typing trainee errors and designing amendment scenarios.
- The CAP/ADASP guideline on interpretive diagnostic error reduction (five recommendations: review selected cases to detect disagreement, timely review, documented procedures, continuous monitoring, act on poor agreement within a case type) is a **published society guideline**, not a regulatory requirement (Nakhleh et al., Arch Pathol Lab Med 2016;140(1):29-40, PMID 25965939 **[PM]**). It supports presenting agreement by case type before by resident.
- Service-model studies suggest discrepancy and consult rates differ by case type and sign-out model (Conant 2017, PMID 28815203; Liu 2016, PMID 27649949 **[PM-note]**); resident-level variation by organ system is an inference, not a finding of those papers.

## 3. Validity, reliability, standards and governance

### 3.1 Frameworks and what a simulation may claim

- Validation is evidence for an interpretation-use argument; Messick's sources (content, response process, internal structure, relations to other variables, consequences) and Kane's inferences (scoring, generalisation, extrapolation, implications) frame it; an eight-step process begins by making the intended decision explicit (Cook and Hatala, Adv Simul 2016, PMID 29450000 **[PM]**).
- The O-SCORE's Kane-ordered systematic review found scoring, generalisation and extrapolation well supported in surgery but "a notable lack of implications evidence" on summative decisions and thin evidence once the tool is reduced to a single global item (Spencer et al., Adv Health Sci Educ 2022, PMID 35511356 **[PM-note]**). O-SCORE has validity evidence on a simulated bench station with blinded raters (MacEwan et al., Teach Learn Med 2016, PMID 26787087 **[PM]**); OCAT re-used in internal medicine gave G = 0.92 with scores rising by training level (Halman et al., BMC Med Educ 2018, PMID 30236097 **[PM]**; Rekman et al., J Surg Educ 2016, PMID 27052202 **[PM]**). None is validated for interpretive pathology tasks in simulation.
- One pharmacy-student study found OSCE scores far above real-world mystery-shopper scores on the same checklist (median 80.9 % vs 41.7 %, 92 visits; Lim et al., Adv Health Sci Educ 2024;29:859-878, PMID 37728720 **[PM-note]**): a caution, not a general finding about simulation.
- Purpose (formative to summative), stakes and process are separate dials to align deliberately (Kinnear et al., Acad Med 2021, PMID 34183603 **[PM-note]**).
- Known-groups differences are not guaranteed: a Kane-framed exam validation found no novice/intermediate/expert difference and still supported a narrow purpose (Gerretsen et al., Respiration 2025, PMID 40505649 **[PM-note]**).

Tiered claims, with what each needs (author inference from the above): **T1** feedback to the trainee (supported by design); **T2** programme and curriculum evaluation (plausible with the cohort CSV plus difficulty stratification, small-cell rules and consent); **T3** one input to the CCC (needs generalisation and extrapolation evidence, file integrity and identity assurance); **T4** entrustment of an activity (not supportable from simulation alone; the simulation is corroborating evidence for a faculty decision on real practice).

### 3.2 Reliability: how many observations

| Study | Setting | Variance and reliability | Observations needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dunne et al., J Gen Intern Med 2022;37:2684-2690, PMID 34561828 **[PM]** | 5 core EPAs, medicine clerkship, 1,368 ratings on 94 students | Person variance "high for all EPAs"; lowest for note writing (9.8 %), which was sensitive to case complexity | 9–11 per EPA for 0.7 |
| Ryan et al., Acad Med 2021, PMID 33464735 **[PM]** | Core EPAs across clerkships, 10,680 assessments on 220 students | Student 3.5–8 %, rater 29.6–50.3 % | 25–63 for Φ > 0.70 |
| Kelleher et al., Acad Med 2020, PMID 31567170 **[PM]** | Internal medicine residency, 166,686 ratings | Rater the largest component (37 % time-specific, 23 % longitudinal); resident 19 % | 0.40 for a typical month; 0.63 over 36 months; 0.76 projected with doubled raters and assessments |
| Nixon et al., Acad Med 2026, PMID 41812055 **[PM]**; Lister et al., J Gen Intern Med 2026, PMID 41612117; Tessmann et al., GMS J Med Educ 2024, PMID 39711857 **[PM-note]** | Pre-clerkship, clerkship, anaesthesia | Learning curves follow a negative exponential with EPA-specific slopes (Nixon) | 4 raters × 4 occasions; 5 × 5; 12 assessments |

Reading: in two of the three largest studies rater variance exceeded person variance; Dunne found person variance high but case-sensitive for note writing. Reference-based automatic scoring removes the rater term for objective items; case variance remains (WIDI: 28.5 %), so difficulty-adjusted scoring and many cases are needed, and the required count must be measured in a G-study on pilot data.

### 3.3 Rater training and bias

- Frame-of-reference training is reported by faculty to improve direct observation and the use of entrustment as the standard, with barriers of holistic preference and culture (Kogan et al., Med Educ 2015, PMID 26077217; Kogan, Conforti, Holmboe, J Grad Med Educ 2023, PMID 36817545 **[PM-note]**; perception studies).
- Training can fail: an 8-hour rater-training programme for a behavioural marker system (anaesthesia non-technical skills, video rating) did not reach r > 0.7 (Graham et al., Br J Anaesth 2010, PMID 20190257 **[PM-note]**). Not an entrustment-scale study.
- Human entrustment ratings carried a gender gap in a simulated randomised study (2.29 vs 2.54 after correcting for scripted performance; Heath et al., J Gen Intern Med 2024, PMID 38289461 **[PM]**). The human baseline is not neutral.

### 3.4 Standard setting and item analysis

- No primary standard-setting methodology source was retrieved; Angoff, Hofstee, bookmark and borderline methods are named from general knowledge **[unverified citations]**. Borderline regression needs an examiner global rating per performance (used in a virtual OSCE; Asmar et al., BMC Med Educ 2025, PMID 40474168 **[PM-note]**).
- CAMELYON17: all algorithms "struggled with reliably identifying isolated tumor cells" (detection below 40 %; Bandi et al., IEEE Trans Med Imaging 2019, PMID 30716025 **[PM]**), which supports treating ITC calls as a difficulty stratum. Residents' ITC detection rates and inter-pathologist agreement on pN **[unverified]**.
- Multidimensional Elo calibrates item difficulty about as well as multidimensional IRT except for very hard items; under between-item structures fully adaptive selection degraded calibration, so a random seeding phase may be needed in those cases (psychometric simulation, not education data; Zhang et al., Front Psychol 2026, PMID 42920583 **[PM]**). IRT, Bayesian knowledge tracing and many-facet Rasch in EPA assessment: no retrievable primary source **[unverified]**.

### 3.5 Programmatic assessment and consequences

- Programmatic assessment: many low-stakes data points, aggregated for high-stakes decisions; twelve Ottawa 2020 consensus principles (Heeneman et al.; Torre et al., Med Teach 2021–2022, PMIDs 34344274, 34330202, 35701165 **[PM-note]**; consensus, not regulation). Learners may read formative assessment as summative, which reduces its feedback value (Watling and Ginsburg, Med Educ 2019, PMID 30073692 **[PM-note]**).
- ACGME's own position that Milestones are primarily formative and not a sole basis for high-stakes decisions **[snippet from ACGME PDFs; not quoted until read]**.

### 3.6 Learning-record standards and residency-management systems

- **cmi5** requires an LMS launch (`fetch`, `actor`, `registration`; "Other launch environments are not currently implemented"), so a standalone tool cannot be cmi5-conformant **[github, read in full]**. **xAPI** statements (actor–verb–object with result and context) go to a Learning Record Store **[github]**; IEEE 9274.1.1-2023 status **[snippet]**. **MedBiquitous** Educational Achievement is described as able to document entrustment decisions and milestone achievement **[snippet]**; vendor uptake **[unverified]**. **1EdTech QTI 3.0** results reporting is for QTI-delivered items and does not fit **[snippet]**.
- **MedHub** publicises an API with specifications released on request and imports of ACGME survey, case-log and milestone data; **New Innovations** imports ACGME case logs; no public documentation of either accepting third-party assessment records was found **[snippet, unverified]**.

### 3.7 Privacy, consent and research governance

- **FERPA and residents is institution-specific.** One UT System institution's policy excludes School of Medicine house staff from "student" (Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center, HSC OP 77.13); Baylor treats residents and fellows as employees **[snippet]**; the other UT institutions' policies **[unverified]**; 34 CFR 99.3 not read.
- **Common Rule exemption (d)(1)** covers normal educational practices in established settings; a 2018 SACHRP attachment says radically new or innovative practices may not qualify and random assignment to curricula probably does not; determinations are local **[snippet]**. Whether a novel simulated assessment qualifies is uncertain; an IRB determination is needed before research use.
- **International partners:** EU–US Data Privacy Framework adequacy (10 July 2023) applies to certified recipients; GDPR Article 89 encourages pseudonymisation **[snippet]**; pseudonymised trainee codes remain personal data where the programme can re-link them (inference). Partner jurisdictions unknown.
- Power imbalance: programme directors who collect files also assess trainees; an honest-broker data steward outside the training chain is an inference-based recommendation.

## 4. State of the art for automated assessment and educational activities

### 4.1 Automated scoring of structured and free-text content

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Rubric scoring of 30 pharmacology short answers: correlation 0.93–0.96 with examiners, ICC 0.94; final score the mean of five sampled runs; tiny single-course sample | Seneviratne and Manathunga, BMC Med Educ 2025, PMID 40764994 **[PM]** |
| 902 responses, 12 short-answer questions, three human graders: human–human QWK 0.90–0.92; best models 0.88–0.90; other models 0.77–0.79 and below 0.63; best-model intra-rater consistency QWK 0.93; "agreement alone does not ensure educational validity"; call for multicentre validation and safeguards for bias, privacy and model drift; models "should augment rather than replace" faculty | Sidali et al., JMIR Med Educ 2026, PMID 42970784 **[PM]** |
| Open-ended exam scoring at two US schools, rubric engineered over three iterations: weighted kappa 0.94 and 0.88 for two analytic rubrics, 0.89 and 0.54 for two holistic ones. The lesson is iterative rubric engineering, not "analytic beats holistic" (one of two holistic items matched the analytic ones). Two authors are employees of the platform vendor | Olvet et al., Med Sci Educ 2026, PMID 42438518 **[PM]** |
| Model-generated rubric from source articles, 165 ethics responses: model–rater agreement 0.77 and 0.68 against 0.57 between the two humans (best of three models); authors insist on faculty oversight | Cole et al., Acad Med 2025, PMID 40865045 **[PM]** |
| Open-weight model extracting 30 variables from 947 consultant-written Dutch brain-MRI reports: balanced accuracy 0.82–0.96 for categorical fields; counts scored by accuracy 0.66–0.80, lifted to 0.81–0.92 by few-shot examples; lesion-location text similarity 0.95, with the authors noting remaining challenges for location-specific variables. Says nothing about grading trainee-written reports | Mouheb et al., Eur Radiol 2026, PMID 42613470 **[PM]** |
| Narrative-comment quality (QuAL) auto-scored within one point of humans in 87 % of instances; applied to 12,841 surgical EPA assessments: 56 % had a comment, 29 % a suggestion, 26.5 % linked it to behaviour | Spadafore et al., Acad Med 2024, PMID 38232079; Willis et al., J Surg Educ 2026, PMID 42715712 **[PM]** |
| Rank-order agreement with a large level offset in reflective-assignment grading (ρ 0.68–0.99; d = 3.4) | Faysal et al., Pak J Med Sci 2026, PMID 42291586 **[PM-note]** |
| No retrieved study validates automated scoring of a trainee's pathology or radiology diagnostic report; the mature radiology simulation grades free text by faculty | PMIDs 39549206, 32376185 **[PM]**; **[unverified: absence of evidence]** |

Safeguards consistent across the studies: itemised rubrics iteratively engineered against faculty scores; several sampled runs averaged; a pinned model, prompt and rubric with re-validation on every change; human oversight for ethics and bias; a viewpoint on AI-assisted formative assessment lists hallucination, automation bias, overtrust, privacy and inequity (Nguyen et al., JMIR Med Educ 2026, PMID 42241685 **[PM-note]**). Grader language bias against non-native phrasing rests on one preprint (arXiv 2603.18765; penalties of 0.90–1.35 on a 10-point scale with two open models, 180 responses) **[snippet, unreviewed]**.

### 4.2 Automated or data-driven entrustment estimation

- No body has adopted automated entrustment decisions **[unverified: absence of evidence]**. The AI-entrustment framework proposes judging the AI tool's trustworthiness by ability, integrity and benevolence with an AI-specific entrustment scale (Gin et al., Acad Med 2025, PMID 39761533 **[PM]**; proposal).
- NLP on 594 end-of-rotation texts for 24 residents estimated CCC milestone ratings with AUC 0.83 (text only) to 0.87 (combined), offered as a way to focus faculty attention (Abbott et al., J Surg Educ 2021, PMID 34266789 **[PM]**). A model assigning milestone levels from faculty feedback correlated strongly with the CCC for four of six competency domains in 24 residents, with no apparent gender or word-count bias at that size (Partin et al., Fam Med 2025, PMID 40663416 **[PM]**; feasibility). A deep network linked entrustment levels to the concreteness of feedback in 3,328 clerkship observations (Gin et al., Med Educ 2022, PMID 34773415 **[PM]**; analysis tool).
- CCC-assigned EPA levels predict CCC-assigned milestone levels with external correlations 0.68–0.72 in 4,328 paediatric residents; the authors call this strong and enabling (Schumacher et al., Acad Med 2025, PMID 40643399 **[PM]**). This is a human-to-human mapping, not an automated estimate.
- Reading (this note's opinion): automated estimates of CCC output are moderately accurate and would reproduce CCC biases; they can triage attention and summarise evidence, not decide.

### 4.3 Viewing-behaviour analytics

- 140 pathologists, 14 breast WSIs: 30 zoom/pan features classified accurate versus inaccurate diagnoses with accuracy 0.81 and AUC 0.86; attention distribution and focus on critical regions were top predictors (Brunyé et al., JAMIA 2024, PMID 38031453 **[PM]**).
- 155 residents, 10 centres, longitudinal: each year of training brought 2 % less time on critical regions, 1 % more coverage, 19 % higher odds of an accurate annotation and 18 % of an accurate diagnosis; detection of critical regions did not change with year; an exploratory analysis made accurate feature annotation the key predictor of diagnostic accuracy. The study did not test coverage or dwell against accuracy (Brunyé et al., Mod Pathol 2025, PMID 40441531 **[PM]**).
- Eye tracking in 92 residents and pathologists: faculty fixated critical regions earlier; no single viewing behaviour was significantly associated with accuracy (Brunyé et al., J Med Imaging 2020, PMID 37476351 **[PM]**). Four residents followed over four years spent less time per slide with fewer fixations (Krupinski et al., Hum Pathol 2012, PMID 22835956 **[PM-note]**). Viewport logs can be turned into candidate regions of interest and weak labels (Mercan et al., 2016 and 2018, PMIDs 26961982, 28981408 **[PM-note]**).
- Reading: viewport tracking without eye hardware is published and predictive at group level; no single behaviour is a reliable individual marker; report behavioural metrics as formative process feedback with uncertainty, never as a competence score or entrustment criterion.

### 4.4 Adaptive, spaced and mastery learning

- RCT, 724 urology residents: daily e-mailed histopathology image cases with feedback versus bolus modules; long-term gain +15.2 % vs +3.4 %; completion 77 % vs 66 % (Kerfoot et al., J Am Coll Surg 2010, PMID 20800189 **[PM]**). Meta-analysis of spaced repetition versus standard study: SMD 0.78 (95 % CI 0.56–0.99), 13 studies, 21,415 learners, mostly text, flashcard and MCQ interventions, so an indirect basis for WSI items (Maye and Hurley, Clin Teach 2026, PMID 41601436 **[PM]**).
- Perceptual and adaptive learning modules: skin histopathology (pre/post gains, retention at 6–7 weeks; Krasne et al., J Pathol Inform 2013, PMID 24524000 **[PM]**); dermatology morphology (effect sizes 1.1–2.2; one author founded the company holding the adaptive-learning patents **[snippet for the conflict detail]**; Rimoin et al., J Am Acad Dermatol 2015, PMID 25592621 **[PM-note]**); inflammatory dermatopathology patterns in 26 residents (+17.1 points; the module group's extra gain of 10.1 points has a printed CI of −2.5 to 17.8; control did pre/post only; Knapp et al., J Pathol Inform 2022, PMID 36268109 **[PM]**); histology with display time tied to accuracy (129 students, 72.6 % to 95.7 %, no retention test; Parker et al., Acad Pathol 2017, PMID 28725787 **[PM]**). No RCT of an adaptive WSI-based module in pathology residents was found **[unverified: absence]**.
- Mastery learning and competency-based simulation have meta-analytic support for procedural skills (large effect on skills, small on practice and patient outcomes; Vermylen et al., Simul Healthc 2025, PMID 41217357 **[PM]**; McGaghie et al., Med Educ 2014, PMID 24606621 **[PM]**). Transfer to cognitive-perceptual sign-out is untested **[unverified]**.

### 4.5 AI-generated feedback and image interpretation

- RCT, 129 first-year students: model-written feedback on clinical-reasoning items equalled expert feedback on scores immediately and at 10 days (no equivalence test), experts were better on complicated cases (p < 0.001), and disclosing AI authorship raised students' critical attitude (Çiçek et al., Postgrad Med J 2025, PMID 39656920 **[PM]**).
- Multimodal models localising findings on chest radiographs: general-purpose models 39.1–49.7 %, a domain-specific model 17.7 %, a task-specific CNN 59.9 %, a radiologist 80.1 % (Gosai et al., Proc Mach Learn Res 2025, PMID 42231937 **[PM]**). Any inference to pathology whole-slide images is an extrapolation. A preprint on navigating gigapixel pathology images with a multimodal model reports 62.5 % on pathologist-written questions (arXiv 2511.19652 **[snippet]**); nothing retrieved says such models fail to link findings to diagnoses.
- Reading: model-generated feedback must be anchored to deterministic evidence (reference, deposits, trace, the learner's own entries), must quote it, and must not interpret images freely.

## 5. Implications (the design lives in plan section 14)

1. **Automate evidence, not trust.** Deterministic reference-based scoring for structured content; rules plus an optional constrained language-model mapper for free text, with the mapping shown to the learner and a deterministic fallback; model-written narrative feedback unscored and evidence-anchored. The entrustment and Milestones decisions remain the CCC's by policy, fed by an auto-generated per-cycle summary and an exception-based queue with a small random audit.
2. **Say which tier the data supports and label every export with tier, purpose and decision-maker.** T1/T2 today; T3 only after generalisation, extrapolation and integrity evidence; T4 never from simulation alone.
3. **Build the mechanics that make it practice, in the order of value for cost:** confidence and a would-I-issue-this choice; deferral, request and consultation with authored replies; a preliminary report, an attending note and a final report or addendum; a free-text diagnosis line with a checklist rubric; blueprinted worklists with normals and a second case type; triage and workload as exploratory measures; a scripted communication station; quality traps; history variants randomised.
4. **Keep what the current build gets right:** irreversible submission, no reference before submission, classes with direction, trace-based miss types, complete/examined flags, derived-versus-entered pN, no composite score, versioned exports, immediate feedback, FR-R0.
5. **Plan the validity programme the grant will promise:** reference adjudication by two faculty; a G-study and D-study to set the case count; known-groups by training year; concurrent validity against RISE and CCC levels framed as association; consequences interviews; a 150–300-report two-rater calibration set for any free-text grader with QWK/ICC and an error taxonomy; pre-registered subgroup audits; pinned grader versions and rubric hashes published.
6. **Governance before data:** separate research consent, IRB determination per site, honest-broker steward, small-cell suppression, data-use agreement, FERPA status asked per GME office, programme-observed sessions or server receipts before any T3 use.
