## Title

AI-agent-built, in-house-verifiable open-source whole-slide image viewing: a capability and cost demonstration on public de-identified data

## Body

**Background:** Whole-slide imaging serves primary diagnosis in a minority of US practices; cost and integration burden are reported barriers, and viewer prices and performance data are rarely public. Can AI coding agents, with humans only as gate reviewers, build an open-source viewer whose performance, provenance and cost hospitals can verify?

**Design:** A multi-agent pipeline wrote 18 research notes, five judge-scored architecture proposals and a plan with machine-checkable gates; 24 load-bearing claims were independently re-derived. Agents built a web prototype (TypeScript/WebGL2) with a level-of-detail and prefetch rendering policy over a static tile pyramid of a 15,360²-px region of one CC0 CAMELYON16 slide (1,210 JPEG tiles). It logs per-frame drawn versus wanted level and per-gesture renderer-reported time-to-sharp (in-page proxy). A scripted five-burst camera path ran from an empty cache on a high-end foldable Android phone (unfolded, treated as a tablet; three runs each over Wi-Fi and cellular). A ledger priced every agent run from its transcript; tile builds were byte-identity checked.

**Results:** On the phone (preliminary; 15 zoom-in bursts per network), median (maximum) time-to-sharp from last input was 246 (380) ms over Wi-Fi and 386 (1,346) ms over cellular; sharp-after-settle (from camera stop, ~0.65 s after input) was 0 ms in every Wi-Fi burst and at most 749.6 ms over cellular. Of frames during and after gestures, 79.9% (Wi-Fi) and 64.2% (cellular) drew no coarser-level tile; none was blank after the base level loaded. The project to date consumed 213 agent runs, 20.65 million uncached tokens and at least US$2,641.53 at list price over 11 days (human hours unlogged); 4 of 24 verified claims held unchanged, none were refuted. Licence scanning found 0 non-permissive client dependencies; a software bill of materials was generated.

**Conclusion:** An AI-agent-built open prototype viewer reached preliminary median time-to-sharp of 246 ms (Wi-Fi) and 386 ms (cellular) on one high-end phone, with machine-checkable provenance. This is a capability and cost demonstration on public de-identified data, not a diagnostic validation; clinical use requires CAP validation and distribution requires FDA clearance.
