    Design rationale · Concept B “Workbench” · 2026-11-01

    What this concept does differently. Concept A hides everything until summoned; Concept B keeps a workbench open: an Explorer on the left (case & identity, slides, layers & AI, bookmarks), an Inspector on the right (clinical context, measure & annotate, compare & sync, case analytics, consult, sign-out hand-off, keyboard map) with an activity bar that switches its panels, and a Ctrl/Cmd-K palette that reaches every command, slide, bookmark, class and layout preset from one keystroke. The bet is learnability and completeness: a resident who has used VS Code, QuPath or Sectra recognises the shape in seconds, and nothing is more than one visible click or two keystrokes away. The cost is the obvious one named in the brief, chrome eating the canvas on laptops, and the concept answers it with a mechanical rule rather than good intentions.

    The three ideas to judge

    1. A canvas-floor governor (P1 made executable). The shell knows the predicted canvas share for any dock configuration. When a request would take the canvas below 70 % of the window, the governor degrades the least important chrome first: the Explorer narrows to a slide strip (identity thumbnail, accession and the three slide thumbnails never disappear), and if that is still not enough the Inspector floats over the canvas edge instead of docking. The dock header states what happened and why (“Narrowed to the slide strip so the canvas stays ≥ 70 %… U restores it”). The review header shows the measured share live; try the Open → Inspector (governor) demo at 1440 × 900 and at 1920 × 1080 and watch the readout. Resizing a dock by hand is never fought, but the dock says when you have gone below the floor.
    2. One command registry. Rail labels, the palette, the keyboard map, the activity bar, the strip chips and the demo strip all dispatch the same serialisable command ids (runCmd), the plan's command bus. The keyboard map is generated from the registry (filterable, names the preset and the reserved dictation/F-key/Alt/Ctrl sets, greyed), so it cannot drift from the bindings. The palette lists slides with their viewed state, bookmarks, annotations and layout presets, which keeps the rail to twelve tools.
    3. Panels are the dialogs. Sign-out hand-off, Request consult, the Dx gate and the keyboard map are Inspector sections (or dock-anchored popovers), never modal boxes in the canvas centre. They can stay open while the pathologist keeps screening: the hand-off section updates its “2 slides not opened” warning as slides are viewed; the consult section shows exactly what the link carries (Tier 0, 24 h, audit) before it is sent.

    Identity, product and mode (P4, P5)

    • The Case & identity section is pinned: it can be compacted (strip, ribbon) but never hidden; the section menu says so. In Dx mode the label image is not collapsible (L says why). Identity is also in the top bar (≥ 1280 px), in the ribbon (< 1280 px), as the HUD identity line and in the navigator label.
    • Badge, full label string (with the lab name as a build-time constant; Design note D2) and the NAV/DX indicator sit outside every dismissible layer; the popovers are anchored to the dock columns and inset from the three top-bar slots. The RUO build removes every data-clinical element (patient block, purpose chip, mode slot, QA and mic chips, sign-out and consult) rather than greying them.
    • The Dx gate is a checklist, not a yes/no: build, window, device tier, display attestation, display QA, deployment self-check, each with its state and the first failure's exact refusal string from principles §5.4. The strip and the live region repeat the same string.

    Degraded states (P3, P9)

    Cold 4G → Soft — slow link half-dot, chip and a soft canvas; offline with pack → pack chip, Sharp; offline without pack → Offline — cached tiles only; slow translator → chip + Sharpening…; tile errors → three blurred ancestor tiles outlined, 3 tiles could not be loaded — retrying (in Dx this becomes Not complete — do not interpret); manifest 503, session expired, identity mismatch (blocking plate in clinical builds, banner in RUO), wrong-product launch and GPU context loss each use the principles §5.6 microcopy. The canvas is never blank: plates sit over a dimmed thumbnail and the identity header stays.

    Accessibility

    • Every accordion header is a real button[aria-expanded]; the tray, annotation list, strip and palette use the listbox/combobox patterns; dock resizers are role="separator" with arrow-key, Home/End and double-click-to-reset; the activity bar is a toolbar with pressed state.
    • SC 2.5.7: navigator pan buttons, click-to-recentre, two-click measure, click-to-place vertices, Space fly-to from any list. SC 2.5.8: 24 px minimum, 44 px under coarse pointers (bottom bar, sheets). All chrome text ≥ 4.5:1 on the neutral greys from the design system; no state by colour alone (filled/ring/half/check/square-with-! plus text).
    • Two live regions: polite for magnification, position, slide and tool changes (debounced 500 ms, spoken at settle); assertive for Dx dropped, identity mismatch and session expiry, using the same strings as the strip.

    Trade-offs the judges should weigh

    • More chrome at rest than A or C. On a 1080p desktop one dock is always open; the governor keeps the floor but the canvas is 75 %, not 85 %. The brief's risk is real and the mitigation is the mechanism above, not a smaller UI.
    • Two docks means two places to look. The split is by what (Explorer: what is in the case) versus how (Inspector: what you are doing to it). Sections can be moved between docks from their menus, so a lab or a user can collapse the model to one dock; the layout chip remembers presets per surface.
    • Floating Inspector overlays the canvas edge on laptops. It stays out of the central 50 % by construction (width ≤ 288 px of 1440) and closes with Esc, but it does cover tissue at the edge while open. The alternative, a bottom sheet, costs vertical space the laptop has less of.
    • Palette discoverability relies on the search box in the top bar and the rail button; first-time users who never press Ctrl-K still have the activity bar and the rail, so nothing is palette-only.

    Design notes (conflicts flagged, not decided here)

    • DN-B1 The plan fixes the dock as one region (D, right side). This concept uses two docks; if the final screen keeps two, principles §3.1's region table needs a seventh region or a note that D may be split. Pillars still “add panels, never canvas behaviour”.
    • DN-B2 The 70 % floor is applied to the app window, with floating docks not subtracted (they overlay the edge). Whether a floating dock counts against P1 should be settled in the critique; the readout in the header makes either reading measurable.
    • DN-B3 U / Shift+U / Shift+P are new bindings for the docks and layout presets; they avoid Ctrl/Alt/F-keys and do not collide with map A/B, but they are not in principles §4.5 and must be filed with the registry (D15).
    • DN-B4 The offline-pack string for a clinical consult pack (Offline pack · AP-26-04417 consult pack · saved …) follows the Learn pattern in §5.5; the clinical wording is not in the plan and needs the microcopy owner's sign-off.
    • DN-B5 The frozen-section variant reuses the purpose chip Consultation · preliminary only plus a TAT chip (Frozen · OR 7 · mm:ss) and a worklist state Frozen · in progress; the plan's fixed purpose strings do not include “frozen”, so this is a proposal.
