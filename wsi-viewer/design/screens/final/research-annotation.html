Design decisions · final research annotation workspace · what came from where, what the judges disagreed on, the engine-contract map

1. The verdict and the shape of the final

The weighted ranking put B Workbench first (7.73), C Task-flow a close second (7.60) and A Canvas-maximal third (6.63); two judges (pathologist, UX/a11y) voted C the winner and the engineering judge voted B. The final is therefore B's structure (two docks with fixed homes, an always-visible search field, the rail with panel switchers, docks as fixed grid tracks resizable by keyboard, the laptop panel strip, the widget as the same shell with docks removed) carrying C's safety scaffolding and geometry (cohort progress in the top bar, To do / In progress / Done, coverage, the candidate-region queue, "Mark slide complete", in-dock keymap and About that never cover the slide, the in-flow sheet on tablets and phones, the canvas input pattern, the explicit export targets, the full degraded-state inventory, whole-canvas CVD simulation, the Clinical-IH / Dx labelling) and A's collapsed-chrome mode (focus mode \ hides rail and docks and shows the HUD tool line and the counted class legend; the tool wheel at the pointer; the inline typed-zoom field; the quality sheet; the per-device channel-cap reasons; the first-run hint retired by commandInvoked).

Why this reconciles the three verdicts: the pathologist would refuse C's step-locked tool rail as a daily driver unless all-steps mode were the default, and C in all-steps mode is a conventional dock with a progress rail, which is B plus progress scaffolding. The UX/a11y judge wanted C's accessibility work (listboxes, radiogroup classes, native range curtain, non-modal keymap, 44 px coarse targets, 4K text scaling), all of which fits B's panels without changing B's IA. The engineering judge wanted B as the thinnest shell over the contracts and C's measured gesture discipline; both are here.

3. Where the judges disagreed, and how it was decided

  • Base concept: B or C. Pathologist and UX/a11y chose C for its safety scaffolding and accessibility; engineering chose B as the thinnest shell; the weighted score chose B. Decision: B's IA with C's scaffolding grafted in, because the pathologist's own condition ("all-steps as the expert default") makes C's rail a progress indicator, and a progress chip plus a cohort panel carries that information with less chrome. The step metaphor itself (one panel per step, tools nested under the step) is not kept: experts interleave Examine and Annotate constantly, and the multiplex panel was a level too deep in C.
  • Sheet on tablet and phone: in-flow or overlay. Pathologist and UX/a11y preferred C's in-flow sheet because the scale bar and magnification stay visible and nothing covers the slide centre; engineering preferred A's and B's overlay sheets because an in-flow sheet resizes the WebGL canvas on every expand (a swapchain resize and tile refetch, one dropped frame on W60). Decision: in-flow, because the expand is a user-invoked re-layout between gestures and the shell can defer it while is-gesturing is set; measured against NFR-P9 as D-F2.
  • Keymap placement. A and B centred a wide dialog over the canvas; C put it in the dock. Decision: C's in-dock overlay everywhere; at laptop widths it opens the drawer, in sheet mode it expands the sheet. Nothing in the dismissible layer may reach the canvas centre (P1 "including dialogs").
  • Palette placement. B centred it under the top bar (into the central band on 1080 px screens); C anchored it under the Commands field at the right edge. Decision: the search field sits at the right end of the top bar and the palette drops over the Context column; in focus mode it stays within the right 25 % of the canvas.
  • Canvas share at 1920 × 1080. B measured 68 % against the viewport (with the review chrome in the measurement) and 72 % against the product frame; C's measured 66 % against its claimed 71 %. Decision: the demo readout reports both, the P1 test applies to the product frame (top bar to strip), and the Context dock is 360 px at 1600–3199 px so the product frame stays above 70 % with the dock open. Pinning the Library at 1920 drops the share to about 61 % and is a user choice reported honestly (B-3).
  • Proposed bindings. A proposed seven new keys (K, ⇧K, U, ⇧E, ⇧G, ⇧V, backtick), B two (\, ⇧G), C three (N, ⇧N, \). Decision: four proposals survive: \ focus mode (B-1), ⇧G channel grid (B-2), ⇧V tool wheel (A), N mark slide complete and open the next to-do slide (C-2; shared mental model with Learn's "next"). Panels are reached through the rail, the strip and the palette, not through letter keys.

8. Known limitations of this mock

  • The tissue is an SVG pattern; at 20–40× it looks like a pattern, not tissue. The zoom demo exists to show the HUD, LOD and sharpness language, not pixels. The detection layer is 3,200 generated centroids standing in for 1.24 M.
  • Pinch, rotate and pen pressure are not simulated; the pen variant is a static illustration of "pen draws, finger pans".
  • Accept / reject, the uncertainty queue, the local tiler and the export jobs are shown as states, not behaviour.
  • Content is synthetic: CAMELYON16 slide names are real public identifiers (CC0); the IHC serial section, the CyCIF field, model names, hashes, the accession AP-26-04417, the patient line DOE, JANE · 1958 and the label image are placeholders (marked data-synthetic).
