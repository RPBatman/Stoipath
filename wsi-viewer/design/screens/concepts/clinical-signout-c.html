      Design rationale · Concept C · Task-flow — key choices, trade-offs, what to look at, engine-contract map

      What this concept does differently

      Concepts A and B organise the screen around objects (the canvas, the panels). Concept C organises it around the task: Open → Screen → Examine → Annotate → Sign-out. A five-step rail (left on landscape, a flow bar at the top on tablet portrait and phone) says where the pathologist is; the dock shows one panel per step with only what that step needs; the tool rail shows only that step's tools. The same five panels are the tab set when the guided flow is switched off, so a power user loses nothing: every tool keeps its key at every step, the palette reaches every command, and ⇧G collapses the rail to dots. The flow is the default reading order, not a wizard: steps can be visited in any order, nothing is locked, and a step that still needs attention (A2-1 unviewed) is marked by shape (square glyph), not colour.

      Key choices

      • Identity is step 1, not a header afterthought. The label image, accession, part/block, patient placeholder, worklist state, launch user and purpose, identity-check result and display attestation sit in one card the reader sees before the first high-power view (P5). The identity line stays on the canvas and the label image stays in the navigator in every step; in Dx mode the label image cannot be collapsed (Design note D13).
      • Screening is a step with a coverage contract. The tray shows viewed / partly viewed / unviewed with a coverage bar per slide, the navigator keeps a viewed-area trail that never auto-fades, and ⇧+arrows pan one full viewport for systematic coverage. What the LIS needs back (viewed state per slide) is the same data the pathologist sees.
      • AI overlays are off by default in Dx and live in the Examine step. Each layer row carries vendor, producer, version, inputHash, createdAt and regulatoryStatus; the research layer carries the fixed FR-R11 marker, the lab-validated layer carries a lab-validated marker with n. AI outlines use palette slots (teal, amber dashed) that the pathologist's own classes do not, so human and machine marks never share a colour. Outline first, heatmap on demand.
      • Sign-out is a checklist, and the viewer never owns the diagnosis. Step 5 lists what returns to the LIS (status, viewed state, measurements as SR, regions as ANN/GeoJSON, AI accept/reject, bookmarks, render mode and display record, audit). The hand-off dialog repeats it, warns about the unviewed slide, and offers to open it first. The report is dictated in Beaker.
      • Pen and touch first, scaling up. On tablet the steps move to a flow bar at the top and the step's tools to a 44 px bottom bar; the step panel is a bottom sheet ≤ 40 % high; pen draws while a finger pans. On the 4K diagnostic display the same rail is 84 px wide with 24 px glyphs and the panel is 400 px: tiny chrome, big canvas (≈ 84 % canvas at rest).
      • Frozen-section consult is the same flow with two steps renamed (Note, Send): a TAT timer against the CAP 20-minute target, preliminary-note templates, a Request-consult affordance (Tier 0 share link, pager), and the fixed Consultation · preliminary only chip. Dx is refused with the Tier-2 string.
      • Hyperdrive iframe: the flow collapses to a chip. No rail, no dock; the step chip opens a sheet; tray chips in the bar; Open on diagnostic display hands off to the companion window; Nav only; the refusal string names the reason. Dialogs and the keyboard sheet render inside the frame (no popups, no top navigation).
      • Honest pixels. The strip and HUD carry Sharp / Sharpening… / Soft — reason / Offline in Nav mode and exactly three Rendering strings in Dx. Cold 4G shows the best resident ancestor (soft) and the link chip; tile errors keep ancestors and say so; the Dx indicator turns to Not complete — do not interpret when tiles are missing. During any drag or wheel the app carries .is-gesturing and no chrome transition runs (P2).
      • Micrometres are the only truth. The scale bar is computed from µm/px and snaps to 1/2/5 × 10ⁿ µm within 80–200 px; the magnification is derived and says its pitch source (EDID / device profile / unknown with a tilde and the Pitch-unknown chip in the iframe).

      Trade-offs and risks (honest)

      • Friction for power users. A step rail is one more thing to read for a pathologist who has signed out 40,000 cases. Mitigations: nothing is gated, every key works at every step, ⇧G turns the flow into plain tabs, and the rail is 72 px (the plan's 56 px rail plus 16). The judges should decide whether the step labels earn their 16 px.
      • Contextual tools hide affordances. Showing only the step's tools keeps the rail short but means a reader in Screen does not see the measure tool (its key still works, and the palette lists it). The flow-off mode shows all tools.
      • Progressive disclosure versus one-glance status. The identity card and the display record live in step 1; between steps, identity is reduced to the top-bar accession, the canvas identity line and the navigator label image. At < 1280 px the QA and worklist chips move into the step-1 panel (design system rule), so on a laptop the QA state is one keystroke away (I), not on screen.
      • The sheet on tablet portrait covers up to 40 % of the viewport while open; it is transient (closes with the grabber, Esc or D) and the canvas is ≈ 86 % at rest.
      • Scale-bar note (Design note). At 20× (0.50 µm/px, DPR 1) a 200 µm bar is 400 CSS px, outside the 80–200 px snap rule, so this mock shows 100 µm at 20× and 200 µm at 10×. The brief's pairing "20× with a 200 µm bar" cannot hold at DPR 1 under the plan's rule; the owner should confirm which rule wins.
      • Preset ladder (Design note D8). The brief names 4×/10×/20×/40× (microscope objectives); the plan's ladder is 1.25/2.5/5/10/20/40 (derived). The presets follow the plan; "4×" is the 2.5 µm/px point between the 2.5× and 5× presets and is reachable by typed zoom.
      • N / ⇧N for steps reuses the Learn build's tour-step keys, which are free in the clinical builds; ⇧G and D are new bindings and must be filed against the command registry.

      What the judges should look at

      • Press N five times from step 1 and watch the dock, rail and HUD: does the sequence match how a case is actually read (identity → survey → high power → marks → hand-back)?
      • Toggle Guided flow off: is the plain-tabs version a viewer a power user would accept on day one?
      • Switch Variant to the iPad frozen consult at 1024 × 1366: the flow bar, 44 px tools, the Note step, the Request-consult dialog and the Tier-2 refusal.
      • Switch to the Hyperdrive iframe: the chip-only flow and the hand-off button.
      • Switch State through Cold 4G, Tile errors, Dx refused and Identity mismatch: the microcopy is the plan's, verbatim; the canvas is never blank.
      • Open the layers section in Examine: the vendor, status and the fixed marker; toggle the overlay in Dx and check the marker and the sign-out checklist update.
      • Press ? and Ctrl+K: every command has a key without Ctrl/Alt/F-keys; reserved dictation and Hyperdrive keys are struck through.
      • Cover the canvas with your hand: badge, label string and mode indicator stay visible in every state and under every dialog (P4).

      Design notes and conflicts flagged

      • DN-C1 Step keys N/⇧N (free in clinical builds), ⇧G guided toggle and D panel toggle are new bindings; file against the command registry.
      • DN-C2 The scale-bar / magnification pairing in the brief (20× with 200 µm) conflicts with the 80–200 px snap rule at DPR 1; this mock follows the rule.
      • DN-C3 The frozen-section consult variant keeps the Tier-2 label image (in-lab iPad under MDM, role-scoped); a share-link consult would be Tier 0 and show no label image, as the Request-consult dialog states.
      • DN-C4 The dock drawer at 1080–1599 px opens by default on step entry (the design system closes it by default) because the step panel is the concept; it closes with D, Esc or the rail button and never changes during a gesture.
      • DN-C5 Selecting the RUO product shows the launch-refused plate rather than a research workspace: the clinical launch has no home in that build (plan 04 §8, FR-R3).

      Known weaknesses of this mock

      • The tissue is synthetic SVG; tile loading, sharpening and the Dx verifier are simulated by timers, not measured.
      • Rotation, curtain compare, snapshot and the B/C/γ panel are stubs (toasts), and pen pressure is not simulated.
      • Only the lock sync mode is honoured in the compare panes; affine and none are labels.
      • Coverage percentages grow with the trail but are not computed from true viewport union.
