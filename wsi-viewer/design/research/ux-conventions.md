# UX conventions of digital-pathology viewers and adjacent expert tools

Research note for the Stoipath UX/UI phase. Date 2026-10-25. Source of truth for product decisions is the plan (`docs/plan/01`, `02`, `04`, `06`, `12`) and the gap notes (`docs/research/11-gap-1`, `-6`, `-7`); where a convention below conflicts with the plan the conflict is flagged as a **Design note**, not resolved here.

**Verification legend.** `[verified]` = read in this session from the cited primary page (GitHub-hosted sources and search-result text were reachable; most vendor, FDA, PMC, W3C, Adobe, Google and ReadTheDocs hosts refused DNS through the sandbox proxy). `[snippet]` = taken from a search-result excerpt of the cited page, not the full page. `[unverified]` = recollection or product observation that could not be checked today; treat as a hypothesis to confirm on a real install or screenshot before it drives a design decision.

## 1. Layout archetypes

Every clinical viewer surveyed converges on one archetype: a dominant canvas, a thin top bar (case identity, magnification, tools), a collapsible left or right rail (slide tray, case details, annotation list), and a thumbnail/navigator in a corner of the canvas. The variation is in *which* corner, how much chrome is persistent, and whether the tray is a vertical column of slide thumbnails (Aperio "gallery column", Sectra "slide navigator", PathPresenter "slide tray") or a horizontal strip.

## 2. Canvas chrome and magnification conventions

Pathology viewers label zoom by **objective equivalent** ("4×, 10×, 20×, 40×"), either as preset buttons/dropdown (Concentriq [snippet], ImageScope presets [snippet]) or as a live readout. Analysis tools label by **scan-relative factor** (QuPath shows a multiplier of the scan's nominal magnification [unverified for exact formatting]); mapping and design tools label by **percentage** of native pixels (Figma `Shift+0` = 100 %, Photoshop `Ctrl+1` = 100 % [snippet]: https://www.photoshopessentials.com/?p=19884).

Two verified findings make the objective label unreliable: Sellaro et al. 2013 showed that "image resolution and scanning (digitization) magnification can vary greatly between digital pathology systems" and that cellular features change apparent size between microscope and screen (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3779393 [snippet]); Kim et al. 2020 found the on-screen "40× HPF" area is **not** equivalent to the microscope HPF and "changed with monitor size, display resolution, and viewer software" while scanner and scan resolution made no difference (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7737490 [snippet]). The plan already adopts the remedy: µm/px is the only authority, with a derived magnification label and a calibrated HPF-area readout (FR-V9). Expert tools (Figma/Photoshop) keep a *typed* zoom field; pathology viewers rarely do.

**Design note.** FR-V9 says the scale bar is "drawn in the UI layer"; NFR-L2 allows scale-bar numerals rasterised from a glyph atlas in the GPU canvas; the hard constraint says overlays are GPU layers and only "lightweight HUD text" is DOM. The design system should pick one home for the scale bar (recommendation: GPU layer with glyph-atlas numerals, so it never re-lays out the DOM during a gesture) and record the ADR.

## 3. Scale bars and minimaps

The de-facto minimap is a slide thumbnail with a rectangle for the current viewport, click-to-recentre, in a corner of the canvas: OSD `TOP_RIGHT` and auto-fading [verified]; QuPath top-right, click moves the view [verified]; Aperio thumbnail with navigation box [snippet]; Sectra overview rectangle with `Home` reset [snippet]; Techcyte a separate "Overview Image" panel [snippet]. Scale bars sit bottom-left or bottom-right, snap to round lengths, and in microscopy use µm/mm (OpenSeadragonScalebar: metric is the default renderer, bar hidden if `pixelsPerMeter` or `location` unset, `stayInsideImage` discouraged under rotation [verified]: https://github.com/usnistgov/OpenSeadragonScalebar). Maps place scale at bottom-right beside attribution and zoom buttons at bottom-right [unverified exact defaults]. Nobody surveyed shows an HPF-area readout, which FR-V9 adds.

## 4. Slide trays and case navigation

Clinical trays are case-centric and LIS-fed: Sectra and Philips take worklist and case from the PACS/IMS; PathPresenter's cleared tray includes priors and deprecated slides [snippet]; Concentriq adds personalised worklists [snippet]. The tray shows a slide thumbnail (macro), usually the label image on demand (Sectra `Q` opens the slide photo; `I` opens details with the label photo [snippet]), stain name, part/block, and read status. Research tools use projects (QuPath) or data trees (OMERO, HALO Link). Nothing surveyed shows the *LIS worklist state* (e.g. "ready for review", "preliminary given") inside the viewer; the plan's launch-context contract carries it (FR-I1, `caseStatusChanged`), which is an opening.

Three conventions recur: (a) `WASD`/arrows pan and `+`/`-` zoom are near-universal and match NFR-A3; (b) **single-letter tool keys** come from Photoshop/Figma via QuPath, and `Space` as a temporary pan tool is shared by QuPath, Figma and Photoshop; (c) the only clinical vendor documenting *customisable* shortcuts is Proscia, whose stated rationale is letting left-handed users "cluster frequently-used commands on the right side of the keyboard" [snippet]. QuPath's docs also note the classic failure: shortcuts work only when the viewer has focus [verified].

## 6. Pointer, wheel and alternative input

Wheel zoom anchored at the cursor is universal (QuPath explicitly; OSD `zoomToRefPoint`; Figma `Ctrl`+wheel) and matches FR-V1. OSD's mouse defaults are instructive as *anti-patterns* for diagnosis: `clickToZoom=true` (a stray click zooms 2×), `animationTime=1.2 s` spring, `blendTime=0`, `maxTilesPerFrame=1` [verified], which is why the plan benchmarks OSD only in a tuned profile. Three vendors offer **hover/auto-pan** so the hand need not drag: Philips "clickless panning", HALO Link "Ergo mode", Techcyte `L` auto-pan [snippet]. Six-degree-of-freedom controllers are supported by Sectra IDS7, Concentriq (FDA listing) and HALO Link [snippet]; Molin 2015 found six pathologists perceived significantly less workload with the mouse and a 6DOF navigator than with a touchpad (https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/215818/local_215818.pdf [snippet]). Rogers et al. 2025 (38 pathologists and trainees, ten navigation methods) found **63 % would need an alternative to mouse click-and-drag** for effective sign-out, with gamepad, SpaceMouse Pro and large trackball most preferred (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12221461/ [snippet]). FR-V11's gamepad row is therefore not a gimmick.

## 7. Annotation tool palettes and class colouring

QuPath's defaults are the lingua franca of research annotation and travel with its GeoJSON dialect (FR-A2): Tumor (200,0,0), Stroma (150,200,150), Immune cells (160,90,160), Necrosis (50,50,50), Other (255,200,0), Region* (0,0,180), Ignore* (180,180,180), Image (128,128,128); Positive/Negative derive from (200,50,50)/(90,90,180) scaled ×1.25; 1+/2+/3+ from yellow/orange/red bases [verified]: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qupath/qupath/main/qupath-core/src/main/java/qupath/lib/objects/classes/PathClass.java. Two of those pairs (Tumor red vs Stroma green; Positive red vs Negative blue-violet) encode meaning in red–green or in hues that sit on the H&E pink–purple axis, so they fail the plan's CVD and on-H&E distinguishability constraints. Tool behaviour conventions: brush/wand size scales with magnification (QuPath [verified]); `Alt` erases, `Shift` adds; names toggle with `N`; locked annotations via context menu; HALO Link adds a layer selector in the toolbar and sorts layers by hue [snippet]; Slim binds each annotation to a coded "Finding" (SNOMED CT) and stores TID 1500 SR [verified]. The Okabe–Ito palette (#E69F00 #56B4E9 #009E73 #F0E442 #0072B2 #D55E00 #CC79A7 #000000) is the standard CVD-safe qualitative set (https://cmap-docs.readthedocs.io/catalog/qualitative/okabeito:okabeito/ [snippet]) but was never evaluated on H&E; Dalton et al. 2026 (gap-6) show H&E diagnostic information is primarily luminance, which argues for luminance-separated outlines with hue as a secondary cue.

## 8. Overlay and AI-result presentation, regulatory labelling

Public labelling is more conservative than product demos. Paige Prostate's US intended use "indicates the location with the highest likelihood of having cancer after a pathologist's initial review" and is "adjunctive"; the **heatmap** language appears in the EU (CE-IVD) intended use, not the US one (https://amplifiermarketplace.sectra.com/pathology/paige-prostate-detect/ [snippet]; De Novo DEN200080). Slim shows segmentations and parametric maps behind right-panel switches with a collapsible in-viewport legend and click-to-zoom on a segment [verified]. A Paige patent application states that a heatmap overlay "can be obtrusive on relevant areas" and proposes a tissue-map alternative [snippet]: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11887304. Radiology evidence is a warning: only 22.2 % of reporting radiographers agreed with heatmap localisation, yet agreement with the heatmap correlated with trust (https://cora.ucc.ie/items/a160171b-64a0-42cb-ae1a-10e040de85ff [snippet]). Cleared viewers surface *display* validity in-UI (Aperio WebViewer DX automatic and manual monitor validation checks [snippet]; PathAI AISight Dx image-quality acceptance check, K243391 [snippet]) and carry FFPE-only intended-use text (Philips, PathPresenter, Aperio) [snippet]. No surveyed viewer visibly labels an overlay's regulatory status per layer; FR-A5/FR-R11's provenance tuple with "not validated for diagnostic use on this viewer" is novel.

## 9. Multichannel controls

Fluorescence UIs share a per-channel row: colour/LUT picker, visibility toggle, min/max (window) slider, optional gamma, plus additive blending. OMERO.iviewer adds per-channel invert, a histogram that follows the active channel, an interpolate toggle and a crosshair intensity readout [snippet]. Viv/Avivator renders on the client GPU so "color mapping, opacity, channel visibility" update immediately, and adds "magic lenses" (local brightness rescale or channel filter under a movable lens) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9637380 [snippet]). napari exposes contrast limits, gamma, colormap, opacity and blending (`translucent`, `additive`, `maximum_intensity`) per layer [snippet]. HALO Link exposes channel toggles in the clinical-research IMS [snippet]; Slim reports 2026 work on "improved multi-channel coloring/blending" [snippet]. The plan's residency caps (≤3 channels at the 8-bit tier on phones, ≤4 on W-iPad, 8 on desktop) have no UI precedent; the channel panel must say *why* a channel cannot be enabled.

## 10. Side-by-side and synchronised viewing

Synchronised pan/zoom of serial sections is a 20-year-old patent family (US 7233340/7623697: manipulating one image "causes other digitized images to be similarly manipulated" [snippet]); automated rotation-aware alignment of H&E/IHC is US 8755633 [snippet]. QuPath offers a multi-view grid with the active viewer outlined in red and synchronisation that propagates pan and zoom [verified]; OMERO.iviewer views several images "at the same time and synchronize the view" [snippet]; Roche markets a multi-slide canvas; Sectra markets side-by-side stains and comparison with prior images [snippet]. Conventions: the active pane is outlined; sync is a toggle; registration is manual (drag-align) or imported. FR-V5's modes (`lock`, `affine`, `nonrigid`, `none`) and curtain compare exceed every surveyed clinical product.

## 11. Pen and touch on tablets

Evidence is thin. The 2011 iPad frozen-section study found 5/6 readers had difficulty navigating at high magnification with the study software (gap-1 F5). No reachable study compares pen tablets with touchscreens for WSI annotation; the closest, Cazzaniga et al. 2025, found touchpad annotation slowest (47.5 min vs 29.9 min mouse) (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-024-01248-x [snippet]); a radiology stylus study favoured the stylus for fine spicule annotation [snippet]. Map apps define the touch grammar users bring: pinch, two-finger rotate, two-finger drag to tilt, and one-handed **double-tap-and-drag** zoom (https://nextpit.com/google-maps-gesture-controls [snippet]; OSD `dblClickDragToZoom=true` on touch [verified]). The plan's "pen annotates while a finger pans" (FR-V11) follows Apple Pencil note-taking apps rather than any pathology viewer.

## 12. Dark chrome and colour on diagnostic displays

Radiology viewers default to black surrounds ("The dark mode is preselected on the medical viewer", https://www.via.cornell.edu/visionx/simba/settings-med.html [snippet]) because radiographs are dark-background images. AAPM TG18 asks for a luminance ratio > 250 and ambient light below the minimum display luminance by a factor of 1.5; later work recommends 75–150 lux rooms (https://amp.spie.org/news/0238-are-we-in-the-dark-about-reading-medical-images ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17278493/ [snippet]). Brightfield slides are the opposite case: the image is mostly near-white glass, so a black frame maximises the luminance step at the canvas edge and a mid-grey tone is perceived lighter on dark grey than on light grey (surround contrast, US 9466129 [snippet]). Current pathology products use neutral dark-grey chrome rather than pure black (Philips, Sectra, Aperio WebViewer DX) [unverified: from product imagery, not measured]. Colour evidence (gap-1 F4): colour management buys decision speed, not accuracy, and pathologists complain when screen colour does not match the microscope (https://www.leicabiosystems.com/fr-br/knowledge-pathway/authors/lance-mikus/ [snippet, vendor]). Clinical viewers expose brightness/contrast and FR-V14 mandates an on-screen indicator when non-default, which no surveyed viewer shows persistently [unverified].

## 13. Accessibility practice and failures

No surveyed pathology viewer publishes a VPAT or documents screen-reader support [unverified: none found]. Known failures: Blackboard's hotspot question type is inaccessible to screen readers (gap-6 F4), and LMS quiz engines cannot host a deep-zoom region item at all, which forces the LTI-tool route (gap-6 F5). WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 explicitly cites map panning: provide on-screen arrow buttons or another single-pointer alternative to drag (https://tuvalabs.com/accessibility/self-assessment/wcag-2-2/ ; https://at.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/whats-new-wcag-2.2.pdf [snippet]); SC 2.5.8 sets 24×24 CSS px targets [snippet]. Google Maps' keyboard page is the model of a published accessible keymap (arrows, `+`/`-`, `Shift`+arrow, `Ctrl+/` to list shortcuts) [snippet]. OSD's keyboard navigation (`keyboardNavEnabled`, `pixelsPerArrowPress`) exists but is undiscoverable in most deployments [verified defaults]. QuPath's "shortcuts only work when the viewer has focus" is the canonical focus-management bug.

## 14. Education-platform patterns

Virtual slide boxes range from "here are the slides, find the structures yourself" (Iowa Virtual Slidebox: users "are then on their own to locate the structures", https://iamse.org/?p=1916 [snippet]) to annotated, quiz-bearing collections (University of Queensland slidebox with drop-down answer forms; PathPresenter auto-scored quizzes, feedback, CME certificates on passing score, groups with polling, https://news.iapcentral.org/iap-bulletins/iap-bulletins-2020-present/vol-61-no-1-2021/v61n12021s4pathpresenter [snippet]). Guided tours over annotations are proven in IIIF (Storiiies, Exhibit; gap-6 F4). Hide/reveal of diagnoses is implemented as separate "student" and "answer" copies in most platforms [unverified], not as per-layer ACL. LMS embedding is LTI 1.3 in an iframe with third-party cookies blocked on Safari/iPad (gap-6 F5; FR-E4). Learning analytics from viewport logs (Walkowski) is a research pattern no commercial platform exposes to learners.

## 15. LIS launch and EHR-iframe patterns

Every clinical viewer is reached from the AP-LIS worklist by a context URL plus HL7 status sync; the viewer never owns the worklist (gap-7 F2). Epic states Hyperdrive is "a secure, Epic-specific web browser" and "web applications hosted within, or launched from, Hyperspace may require changes to work with Hyperdrive" (https://open.epic.com/Prototype/Hyperdrive [snippet]); embedded pages must send a CSP `frame-ancestors` naming the Hyperdrive/Hyperspace domain (ServiceNow EMR Help doc, https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/AK18yGU6kuNpXDV4oOETsw/aA3FoSld60eOwL5MhbZUOw [snippet]); third-party cookies and silent token acquisition fail inside iframes, so apps fall back to popups or, better, token-in-launch (https://forum.posit.co/t/third-party-cookie-restrictions-along-with-iframe-and-shiny/188989 [snippet]). Popup behaviour inside Hyperdrive is **[unverified]**; Epic's "Prepare Web Content for Hyperdrive" document was unreachable. The plan's companion-window pattern (thin controller iframe hands off to a top-level window or Tauri shell on the diagnostic display, plan 12 §3.3) has no public precedent among vendors; Sectra and Philips achieve multi-monitor placement because they *are* the desktop client.

## 16. What users complain about

- **Navigation is the lowest-rated item.** MSK's remote validation rated "navigation" lowest; a 16-pathologist network scored zoom latency 2.4/3 (gap-1 F2). 63 % of Rogers 2025 participants want an alternative to click-and-drag [snippet].
- **Blur and pixelation are logged as defects.** 1,260 remote frozen reads recorded "blurring" (24) and "pixelated images" (19) as artefacts; one missed focal invasion was attributed partly to "WSI latency" (gap-1 F4). UK multisite reporting: home bandwidth caused "delays and pixelation", one pathologist "had to give up on a case as the slides were loading so slowly" (https://www.pathologyinformatics.org/assets/jpi/January%202022%20Vol%2013%20Issue%204%20%20%20Research%20Article-%20A%20feasibility%20study%20of%20multisite%20networked%20digital%20pathology%20reporting%20in%20England%20copy.pdf [snippet]).
- **Not faster than glass.** Most UK pathologists did not agree digital reporting was quicker [snippet]; median 19 % longer per case (Hanna 2019, gap-1).
- **Physical strain.** Patent filings record "arm and wrist fatigue due to the physical action of moving the mouse" and complaints about switching between pan and rotate modes "by clicking on different buttons or by using right mouse click options" (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12182996 ; https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9760678 [snippet]). Swedish pathologists, by contrast, said ergonomics *improved* after digitisation (Thorstenson 2014, https://doaj.org/article/ff434ba2c91640739a4c8ad51c8c4483 [snippet]).
- **Colour mismatch with the microscope** [snippet, vendor-authored].
- **Eye strain / motion sickness:** no pathology-specific measurement found [unverified]; a SPIE observer model notes finite frame rate and zero-order-hold reconstruction cause visible panning artefacts (https://neurophotonics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10136/101360O/An-observer-model-for-quantifying-panning-artifacts-in-digital-pathology/10.1117/12.2255533.full [snippet]).
- **"40×" means different things on different monitors** (Kim 2020) [snippet].

## Conventions to break deliberately

| Convention | Break because | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Objective label as the *only* magnification semantics | Kim 2020, Sellaro 2013: "40×" is display-dependent | µm/px authority with derived label, HPF mm² readout, and a typed zoom field (Figma/Photoshop habit) |
| Click-to-zoom (OSD default) | Accidental 2× jumps during screening; pathologists click to annotate/select | Single click never changes the camera; double-tap zoom only on touch |
| Blur-then-sharp with spring easing and 1-tile-per-frame rationing | Users log blur as a defect; plan G1 | Destination-level residency, honest sharpness indicator, no spring beyond 80–120 ms log-space easing (FR-V1/FR-V2) |
| Auto-fading navigator | Hides spatial context mid-pan; fade is a DOM animation during gestures | Always-visible navigator, GPU-composited, collapsible by key |
| Modal pan/rotate/annotate switching by toolbar clicks | Patent-documented complaint; one hand must stay on the mouse | Modeless: pan is always drag/wheel; tools are momentary single-letter keys; pen draws while finger pans |
| QuPath default colours (Tumor red / Stroma green; Positive red / Negative violet) | Red–green and on-H&E hues fail CVD and distinguishability | Keep class names, remap colours to a luminance-separated CVD-safe palette validated on H&E; honour QuPath colours only on export |
| Heatmap as the default AI presentation | Obtrusive on tissue (Paige patent); weak localisation trust in radiology | Outline/tissue-map default with heatmap on demand; provenance chip with regulatoryStatus (FR-A5/FR-R11) |
| Dark chrome = pure black | Brightfield slides are near-white; black surround maximises glare/adaptation steps | Neutral dark grey tuned to luminance, not black (measure against TG18-style ambient targets) |
| Separate "student" and "answer" slide copies | Duplicates pixels, leaks answers | Per-layer ACL with reveal-on-event (FR-A1, FR-E7) |
| Viewer-side worklist | LIS is the system of record (gap-7) | Tray reflects LIS-passed state and never edits it |
| Silent degradation (soft tiles, missing display attestation) | Honesty is the plan's differentiator | First-class degraded states with named reasons (NFR-B4, FR-R7, Tier badge) |
| Shortcuts that only work with canvas focus | QuPath/OSD focus bug | Global command bus with a visible focus ring and `role="application"` live region (NFR-A3, FR-V12) |

## Ten design opportunities tied to the plan's goals

1. **Sharpness-honest HUD (G1, FR-V13, NFR-P16).** A one-glyph settled/soft indicator driven by NTF % and resolution deficit, becoming the "Rendering Complete" mark in Dx mode (FR-V4). No competitor shows sharpness state; users currently infer it from blur.
2. **Burst-aware magnification ladder (G1, FR-V9, gap-1 F1).** Presets at 4/10/20/40× equivalents with µm/px and HPF mm² beside the label; wheel bursts snap to the nearest preset at release so the destination level is the one the scheduler prefetched.
3. **Unmistakable product and mode band (FR-R3, FR-I1, FR-V4, FR-I10).** A fixed strip carrying build name (Viewer RUO / Learn / Clinical-IH / Dx), render mode, display-attestation source and tier; the RUO string is a CI-asserted UI element, not a footer.
4. **Identity echo as a layout primitive (FR-R9).** Accession, part, block, slide label text and the Tier-2 label image occupy a fixed slot in clinical builds; a mismatch replaces the canvas content with a blocking state. No surveyed viewer treats wrong-slide as a UI hazard.
5. **Companion-window launch flow (plan 12 §3.3, FR-I2).** The Hyperdrive controller iframe shows LIS worklist state and a single "Open on diagnostic display" action; the top-level window and iframe stay in sync through the launch token. This is the only pattern that gives an iframe-born viewer a diagnostic monitor.
6. **Keyboard-first command bus with exportable keymaps (FR-V12, NFR-A3).** Every action has a shortcut, a `?` overlay lists them, and a left-hand/right-hand cluster preset exists (Proscia's rationale); the same commands serve voice, gamepad and presenter mode, and keys reserved for Dragon Medical One are documented (FR-I9).
7. **Alternative-input ergonomics (FR-V11, Rogers 2025).** Hover/auto-pan ("Ergo mode" class), gamepad and 6DOF mappings shipped as first-class presets; the chrome never requires a drag (SC 2.5.7) so the alternatives are also the accessibility path.
8. **Overlay provenance chip (FR-A5, FR-R11, FR-A4).** Each layer row shows producer/version/regulatoryStatus; in Dx mode non-validated layers render with the fixed "not validated for diagnostic use on this viewer" marker; default presentation is outline/tissue-map with heatmap on demand and accept/reject events.
9. **First-class degraded states (NFR-B4, NFR-P6, FR-R7, tiers).** Bandwidth floor banner (<8 Mbps), tier badge (W60 "60 Hz"), offline-pack indicator, tile-error placeholders that show the ancestor level rather than blank, and a Dx-refusal dialog that names the missing attestation.
10. **Education built on the same chrome (FR-E2, FR-E3, FR-E10, gap-6 F8).** Record-a-tour from live navigation; region-marking items with keyboard placement and a text alternative scored equivalently; a CVD remap toggle plus author-side deuteranopia simulation; learner-visible heatmap-vs-expert view. Blackboard's inaccessible hotspot is the gap to exploit for UT procurement (VPAT).

## Open questions for the owner

1. Which viewer do the pilot lab's pathologists use today (Sectra, Aperio, Philips, Concentriq)? Their current keymap decides which "keep" rows are load-bearing.
2. Primary magnification label: objective-equivalent with µm/px secondary, or µm/px primary? FR-V9 permits either presentation.
3. Default keymap: QuPath-style single-letter tools (research habit) or a clinical set modelled on Sectra/Techcyte? Should the left-hand cluster be the default for a right-handed mouse user?
4. Navigator corner (top-right per OSD/QuPath) and whether it may ever auto-hide on phones.
5. Chrome luminance target: pick a grey (e.g. ~L* 20–25) and confirm against the reading-room ambient light the lab actually has.
6. Label image placement in Clinical-IH: always visible in the rail, or visible on open and collapsible? FR-R9 says "by default"; is collapse permitted?
7. Wheel direction and trackpad pinch defaults; is click-to-zoom disabled everywhere or only in clinical builds?
8. Tablet grammar: pen draws / finger pans as the only mode, or a toggle for pen-pans users?
9. RUO/Learn/Clinical-IH/Dx band: persistent full-width strip (unmistakable, costs ~24 px) or a corner chip?
10. Which keys and microphone states must stay free for Dragon Medical One / VoiceOver PRO (FR-I9)?
11. Should the AI presentation default (outline vs heatmap) be per-algorithm (vendor-specified) or a user preference?
12. Is the HPF-area readout wanted during screening or only when counting (mitoses), i.e. a tool-mode HUD element?
