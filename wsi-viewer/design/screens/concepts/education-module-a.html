Design rationale · Concept A · Canvas-maximal (what the judges should look at, trade-offs, engine-contract map)

The idea in one line

The slide is the UI. Regions C (tool rail) and D (context dock) do not exist at rest: they are folded into two labelled 28 px edge handles, and everything the learner must read lives in exactly one summoned surface, the step card at the bottom centre, which behaves like a caption track (tour text, narration progress, the item engine, feedback). Tools are a transient radial at the pointer; the course shell (module list, review queue, credit, settings) is a summoned right-edge drawer; the stepper is a 4 px rail along the canvas top edge. Only the top bar (badge, Learn string, module and step) and the status strip (sharpness, network, tier, gradebook) stay fixed, because P4 and P9 are not negotiable.

Canvas share at rest: ≈ 93 % at 1920 × 1080 and ≈ 96 % at 3840 × 2160 (top bar 48 + strip 32 are the only fixed chrome); with the step card open the central 50 % × 50 % stays clear on every viewport (toggle the guide in the demo strip to check).

Key choices

  • One reading surface, fixed place. Tour text, item stem, feedback and the reveal all appear in the same card at the same position, so the learner never searches for "where did the question go". The card is also the visual twin of the polite live region: captions and screen-reader text are the same string.
  • Chrome collapses before the canvas does, by default. The dock becomes a drawer summoned by ⇧K (or the Module handle / bottom-bar button); the card hides with K leaving a one-line handle. Panels change only between gestures (.is-gesturing zeroes transitions).
  • Radial at the pointer instead of a rail. Right-click, long-press (450 ms) or Pencil squeeze opens eight tools around the cursor, each labelled with its key; the keyboard path is the key itself or CtrlK. It is a transient, user-invoked layer (the same class as the pen-tip palette in principles §4.4), so it is exempt from the central-zone rule while open.
  • Expert-first, learner-safe. The 4K pathologist gets a 96 % canvas and never leaves the keyboard; the undergraduate still sees two visible handles with their keys, a stepper rail with tooltips, the strip hint "? keys · Ctrl+K commands", and a card that re-summons itself whenever a step with content begins.
  • Degraded states live in the strip and on the pixels, never as plates over tissue. Cold 4G shows the best resident ancestor (softened in this mock) with Soft — slow link; the offline pack is a chip; tile errors keep ancestors and say 3 tiles could not be loaded — retrying; a launch for the wrong product is the only blocking plate, and the LEARN badge stays above it.
  • Author and director reuse the same canvas. Recording a tour is a floating bar over the slide; the item editor and collection live in the same drawer; cohort analytics are a layer over the slide (dwell as luminance, expert path in amber, cohort path in dashed teal) with aggregate-only stats in the drawer.

Trade-offs (honest)

  • Discoverability is the risk. A first-time learner has to notice two edge handles and a 4 px rail. Mitigations: handles are always visible and show their key, every step with content auto-summons the card, the strip names the two meta-keys, and the keymap sheet opens with ?. Needs a formative test with undergraduates before adoption.
  • Hidden hierarchy. The module list and review queue are one key away rather than always visible; a course director who wants to see progress at a glance will prefer a dock-forward concept.
  • Bottom card vs HUD corners. On laptops the card is width-limited (viewport − 360 px) so it never touches the scale bar or the magnification readout; on tablets it becomes a sheet (≤ 40 % height) and the HUD rises above it. This costs vertical canvas on portrait tablets whenever the card is open.
  • Right-click is taken. The radial consumes the context menu over the canvas (the plan already forbids the browser menu there); annotation context actions move into the same radial.
  • Two new keys. K (step card) and ⇧K (module drawer) are additions to the Learn map (Design note, below).

  5. Degraded: cold 4G, offline pack, tile errors, classroom busy, Tier 2 · 60 Hz, launch asks for Dx / Clinical-IH, LTI failed.
  6. Views: Author (recording bar, step transcript editor, region-item editor with publish check and CVD simulation, collection with licence and determination), Director (heatmap vs expert path, aggregate-only guard).
  7. Phone (390 × 844): resident unknown-slide variant, "Record your diagnosis before the reveal", differential chips, optional timer, sharpness dot in the HUD, offline pack chip in the HUD.
  8. Reduced motion: HUD chip "Reduced motion · fly-to jumps"; the narration progress bar is static.

Design notes (flagged for the plan owner)

  • DN-A1 · Two Learn-only bindings. K step card, ⇧K module drawer; both free in the default map; filed against the command registry.
  • DN-A2 · Shift+R. Principles §4.5 binds ⇧R to both "rotate anticlockwise" (map A) and "reveal" (Learn additions). Here ⇧R reveals only while an item is committed and awaiting reveal; otherwise it rotates. The conflict should be resolved in the principles document.
  • DN-A3 · Adapted microcopy. The wrong-product plate and the Shift+D notice reuse the §5.4/§5.6 Viewer strings with "Stoipath Learn, for educational use" substituted; counsel's final strings replace them in one place (the i18n bundle).
  • DN-A4 · Tier chip in Learn. Principles §2.4 shows Tier 2 only when it limits a mode. Learn has no Dx, so the iPad chip here states the limit it does impose (60 Hz cadence); confirm whether Learn should show it at all.
  • DN-A5 · Radial over the canvas. Treated as the same transient class as the pen-tip palette (§4.4). If the owner wants the central-zone rule absolute, the radial can be constrained to open at the nearest edge-safe position.
  • DN-A6 · Mock liberties. Tissue is a synthetic SVG; "soft" is a CSS blur standing in for a magnified ancestor; pins and labels are HTML here but GPU layers in the product; the scale bar is DOM here (Design note D11 stands).
