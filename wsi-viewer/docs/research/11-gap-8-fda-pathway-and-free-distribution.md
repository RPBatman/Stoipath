# 11 — Gap 8: FDA pathway for an open, freely distributed WSI viewer, and the organisation that can hold the clearance

Research note for the Stoipath WSI viewer plan. Date: 2026-10-24. Audience: engineers, technical PO, and (later) a regulatory consultant. Companion notes: 09 (regulatory classes, licences, security baseline), 11-gap-7 (LDT-rule rescission, CLIA remote review, CAP validation, Proscia/PathPresenter clearances). This note goes *deeper* on the device pathway and *wider* on the organisational and financial model behind "an FDA-cleared viewer given free to any qualifying hospital".

**Verification legend.** **[verified]** = read today from a primary source reachable from this sandbox (PubMed records via the PubMed connector, ClinicalTrials.gov via the Clinical Trials connector). **[snippet]** = read from search-engine extracts of the named primary or secondary source; fda.gov, accessdata.fda.gov, ecfr.gov, federalregister.gov, govinfo.gov, innolitics, fda.report, tidepool.org, Texas statutes and nearly every law-firm site return 403/DNS failures from this sandbox, so full texts could not be opened. **[unverified]** = domain knowledge, to be checked by counsel/consultant. **[model]** = our own estimate from stated inputs. Two corrections to note 09: software-only viewers carry product code **QKQ**, not PSY; and the AI regulation 21 CFR 864.3750 carries product code **QPN**, not QKQ.

## 1. Summary

1. **A software-only viewer is its own Class II device, not an accessory.** FDA created product code QKQ, "Digital pathology image viewing and management software", under 21 CFR 864.3700; at least 13 QKQ devices have been cleared since Sectra's K193054 (March 2020), most recently PathPresenter K250968 (June 2025, 81 FDA days, no clinical study), PathAI AISight Dx K243391, Indica HALO AP Dx K252762 and INFINITT DPS K243449 **[snippet]**. The predicate chain is dense, so our pathway is a Traditional 510(k) with a viewer predicate; De Novo is unnecessary.
2. **The special controls are a bench-and-pairing exercise for a viewer, a reader study for a scanner.** 864.3700 requires component- and system-level bench tests (colour reproducibility, spatial resolution, display), a human-factors process, labelling that names the components, and the sentence "It is the responsibility of a qualified pathologist to employ appropriate procedures and safeguards to assure the validity of the interpretation of images obtained using this device" **[snippet]**. Scanner sponsors ran 1,161–2,047-case, 10–19-reader, 3–5-site non-inferiority studies with a 4 % margin; viewer-only sponsors (Sectra, Paige, PathPresenter) cleared with pairings to already-cleared scanners and displays **[verified/snippet]**. Budget the viewer at $0.4–1.2 M, not the $3–5 M a pivotal study costs.
3. **Scanner/display pairing is loosening, through PCCPs rather than through a "display-agnostic" claim.** Proscia's August 2026 510(k) carries a PCCP that lets it add FDA-cleared scanners, formats and displays without new 510(k)s **[snippet, vendor]**, and Leica's GT 450 DX study validated remote (internet) viewing with major-discrepancy 2.17 % remote vs 2.61 % local **[verified]**. The general-device PCCP guidance is still draft (final version on CDRH's FY2027 priority list) **[snippet]**, but FDA is granting them now.
4. **A University of Texas institution can be the manufacturer, and already is one.** McGovern Medical School (UTHealth Houston) is the 510(k) holder for HeartSee cardiac-PET software: K171303 (2017), K202679 (2021), K231731 (2023) **[snippet, FDA database]**. 21 U.S.C. 379j waives 510(k)/De Novo fees and the establishment fee for a State government entity "unless the device involved is to be distributed commercially" **[snippet, statute]**, and the Texas Tort Claims Act caps state liability at $250 k/person, $500 k/occurrence while Texas courts hold that information (including computer output) is not "tangible personal property" **[snippet]**. The price is that a state agency cannot indemnify (Tex. Const. art. III §49) **[snippet]**.
5. **"Free" is legally ambiguous and must be asked of FDA.** 21 CFR 807.3(b) defines commercial distribution as a device "held or offered for sale" **[snippet]**; FDA's position on no-charge distribution could not be found. Either free distribution is not "commercial" (then the government fee exemption applies and, strictly, no 510(k) is required, which is not what we want) or it is (then fees apply and the exemption is lost). A free Pre-Sub resolves this; it also decides documentation level, pairing strategy and PCCP scope.
6. **Open source and clearance co-exist; the precedents are concrete.** Tidepool (501(c)(3)) cleared Tidepool Loop K203689 (Jan 2023) with a public QMS, "qmsOS", ISO 13485-certified by TÜV SÜD in April 2025 and run on Google Workspace, Jira and Confluence **[snippet]**; OsiriX MD (K101342) is the cleared edition of an open-source core whose free fork is not **[snippet]**; OHIF underpins "many ... FDA Cleared" viewers while itself staying uncleared **[snippet]**; and on 3 June 2026 Tempus launched an IMS Open-Source Consortium with Yale New Haven and MSK to open-source the Paige IMS including the FDA-cleared FullFocus viewer **[snippet]**. Clearance attaches to the holder's controlled build, never to the code.
7. **Money: the cleared Dx edition costs roughly $1.5–3 M to reach clearance and $0.4–0.8 M/year to keep, dominated by people, not fees.** FY2027 user fees are $28,653 (510(k)), $7,163 small-business, $13,785 annual registration, $8,596 513(g); Pre-Subs are free **[snippet]**. NCI ITCR (Emory's $3.5 M U24 funded the Digital Slide Archive), CPRIT (SEED up to $3 M; TDDC "no maximum"; Core Facility awards $2–3 M), ARPA-H INDEX and philanthropy (Helmsley's $0.9–3 M grants to Tidepool) are the realistic sources; REDCap's "free to non-profits that sign the consortium licence and self-host" is the distribution template **[snippet]**.

## 2. Findings

### F1. Classification, product codes and the predicate landscape

**Regulation.** 21 CFR 864.3700 (Class II, special controls) was created by the Philips IntelliSite De Novo DEN160056 (April 2017). The identification covers "an automated digital slide creation, viewing, and management system intended as an aid to the pathologist to review and interpret digital images of surgical pathology slides" **[snippet]**. Two product codes sit under it: **PSY** (systems including a scanner) and **QKQ** ("software intended for viewing and management of digital images of scanned surgical pathology slides prepared from FFPE tissue") **[snippet]**. AI that reads slides is a separate regulation, 864.3750, product code **QPN** (Paige Prostate DEN200080, granted 21 Sept 2021) **[snippet]**.

**Special controls (as applied in QKQ/PSY summaries) [snippet]:** indications must name the tissue type (FFPE surgical pathology) and the system components; component-level bench data (slide feeder, light source, imaging optics, display system, "as appropriate"); system-level bench data (colour reproducibility, spatial resolution, focusing, tissue coverage, stitching error, turnaround time); a reader study of major-discordance difference between manual-digital and manual-optical review against the sign-out reference ("as appropriate"); a detailed human-factors engineering process on the UI; 809.10(b) labelling with the mandatory pathologist-responsibility sentence. FDA's 2016 "Technical Performance Assessment of Digital Pathology WSI Devices" guidance is the bench checklist (note 09).

**Intended-use language.** PathPresenter's summary describes "software for viewing and managing whole slide images created from FFPE tissue, as an aid to the pathologist to review and interpret digital images for primary diagnosis; not intended for frozen sections, cytology or non-FFPE specimens; Rx only", with the pathologist-responsibility sentence **[snippet, paraphrased; verbatim text unreadable from sandbox]**. Every QKQ IFU we could see follows the same template: FFPE only, named scanner/file format, named display, Rx, pathologist responsible.

**What the pattern tells us.** (i) The viewer is a device in its own right, so OEM embedding is optional, not necessary. (ii) Viewer-only sponsors have cleared without reader studies when they paired with a cleared scanner and a display and showed pixel-pathway equivalence; the reader-study special control is read as "as appropriate" for a viewer whose predicate already carries the clinical evidence. (iii) The two scanners that appear in almost every pairing are the Hamamatsu S360MD and the Leica GT 450 DX; our first pairings should be those. (iv) Clearance timelines for viewers are short: PathPresenter 81 FDA days; the 2025 industry average total time to decision was ~146 calendar days, median ~142–151 **[snippet, aggregators]**, against MDUFA V goals of 112 days (FY2025) and the 90-FDA-day goal met for 97.9 % of 510(k)s in FY2025 **[snippet]**. Scanner submissions with pivotal data take 9–10 months (S360MD 288 days).

**PCCP status.** The AI-enabled-device PCCP guidance is final (Dec 2024); the all-device draft (Aug 2024) is listed for finalisation on CDRH's FY2027 guidance agenda (published 1 Oct 2026, comments to 30 Nov 2026) **[snippet]**; IMDRF published its PCCP guidance for SaMD in Aug 2026 **[snippet]**. The statutory basis (FD&C §515C, from FDORA 2022) does not depend on guidance **[unverified]**. A viewer PCCP would describe: the pairing-verification protocol (TPA bench tests on the new scanner's files or display: colour, resolution, navigation latency, annotation fidelity), acceptance criteria, labelling update procedure and the commitment to use only FDA-cleared scanners/displays.

**Display-agnostic claims.** No cleared viewer labels "any display" **[snippet]**; Paige's "wide set of monitors" and Leica's validated remote viewing are the furthest FDA has gone. A tablet or home laptop remains outside the Dx label; the engineering answer (note 09) is a Dx-mode gate that refuses to start on an unvalidated display. The interoperability guidance (6 Sept 2017) **[snippet]** is the frame for documenting the DICOMweb/file-format interfaces and their verification.

### F3. Boundary: research and education modules outside the device

- **RUO.** 21 CFR 809.10(c)(2)(i) prescribes "For Research Use Only. Not for use in diagnostic procedures." **[unverified wording]**; FDA's Nov 2013 RUO/IUO guidance says the label alone does not control, FDA looks at the totality of circumstances (marketing, features, customer base), and it has a section on software labelled RUO **[snippet]**. Consequence: an RUO build must differ from the Dx build in more than a flag: no LIS sign-out integration, no Dx-mode display gating, distinct name, distinct website claims.
- **Education.** Software intended for medical training/education is not a device under FDA's device-software policy (the Mobile Medical Applications/Device Software Functions policy lists educational tools as non-device examples) **[unverified]**. The Jan 6 2026 revisions to the CDS and General Wellness guidances (issued without comment period) widened the CDS carve-out but kept the exclusion for software that analyses images/signals **[snippet]**, so no image-viewing function can shelter under CDS. Keep the education module a separate product with its own name and no PHI.
- **In-house clinical use before clearance.** Covered in note 11-gap-7: within one CLIA lab, a lab-validated tool under FDA enforcement discretion (LDT rule rescinded 19 Sept 2025), CAP ≥60-case validation per application.

### F4. Quality system and design controls for a software-only manufacturer

**QMSR.** Effective 2 Feb 2026, 21 CFR 820 incorporates ISO 13485:2016 (plus ISO 9000:2015 clause 3) by reference, keeps FDA-specific record and labelling/packaging requirements, renames the DMR the Medical Device File, and FDA inspects under Compliance Program 7382.850 rather than QSIT **[snippet]**. ISO 13485 certification is not required by FDA but is what hospitals and EU/UK regulators ask for; initial certification for a <50-person organisation is quoted at $22–83 k (consultant $5–30 k) **[snippet]**.

**Minimal QMS for a software-only specification developer [unverified structure, standard practice]:** quality manual and ISO 13485 clause map; document and record control (820.35 records); management review; internal audit; training/competence; risk management (ISO 14971) with a hazard list that includes mis-rendered pixels, wrong-slide display, colour shift, stale cache, label leak; design controls with a design history file per release train; software lifecycle (IEC 62304) with SOUP list; usability engineering (IEC 62366-1); cybersecurity plan; supplier controls (cloud providers, codec libraries, scanner vendors as data suppliers); production/release (signed builds, configuration manifests); complaint handling, MDR, corrections/removals, CAPA; UDI/GUDID; labelling. Tidepool's qmsOS shows this can live in Google Workspace/Jira/Confluence **[snippet]**; eQMS products quote $10–30 k/year entry (Greenlight Guru ~$15 k rising to $50–60 k with 2026 price increases; Qualio ~€25 k; Matrix from €10 k) **[snippet]**. OpenRegulatory publishes free ISO 13485/62304 templates **[unverified; site unreachable]**.

**IEC 62304 class and FDA documentation level [analysis].** FDA's June 2023 software guidance replaced Level of Concern with Basic/Enhanced documentation; Enhanced applies when a failure could present a probable risk of death or serious injury before risk controls **[snippet]**. For a primary-diagnosis viewer, a silent rendering fault (missing tile at high power, wrong Z-plane, colour profile dropped) can produce a missed carcinoma; expect **Enhanced** documentation and plan IEC 62304 **Class C** for the pixel pathway, because the pathologist is the user, not an external risk control, and glass is by design not re-reviewed. Use IEC 62304 §4.3 segregation to keep the education/collaboration/research modules at Class A/B with a documented architectural boundary; this is also what keeps the open-source core cheap to change.

**SOUP and platform.** Browsers, OS, GPU drivers, WASM runtimes, codec libraries (note 09 inventory) are SOUP/OTS; the Aug 2023 OTS guidance wants identification, hazard analysis, verification and a maintenance plan **[snippet]**. Practical consequences: pin and hash every dependency, run the golden-image render tests on every supported browser/OS matrix in CI, treat browser auto-updates as an anomaly source monitored post-market, and declare supported platforms in labelling.

**Continuous delivery under design controls.** The Oct 2017 "Deciding When to Submit a 510(k) for a Software Change" guidance **[snippet]** gives the decision tree: changes that could significantly affect safety/effectiveness, or that alter intended use, need a new 510(k); others are documented with a letter-to-file and verification. Operating model that works: a Dx release train (quarterly, full regression, letter-to-file memo per release), an RUO train (continuous), PCCP for pairings, and a defined "significant change" checklist in the QMS. The eSTAR template and Special 510(k) (30-day median in 2025 **[snippet]**) handle the occasional own-device modification.

**AI-written code [analysis].** No FDA document distinguishes code by author; IEC 62304 and the 2023 guidance require requirements, architecture, detailed design, unit/integration/system verification and traceability regardless. Agent-generated code is therefore in-house code with full verification obligations, not SOUP. Record the agent/model version and prompt in the design record, require human review and signed commits, scan for licence-contaminated snippets, and keep deterministic test oracles (golden images) because reviewers will ask how a largely machine-written codebase was verified.

**Cybersecurity.** §524B (cyber devices) requires a cybersecurity plan, SBOM, post-market vulnerability monitoring and coordinated vulnerability disclosure; the governing guidance is now "Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality Management System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions" (3 Feb 2026, superseding 27 June 2025, which superseded Sept 2023) **[snippet]**. Deliverables overlap with note 09's security baseline; the device-specific additions are threat modelling per architecture view, a security risk assessment separate from safety risk, a patch-cadence commitment, and the SBOM in SPDX/CycloneDX with known-vulnerability assessment.

**Human factors.** The "Content of Human Factors Information in Medical Device Marketing Submissions" guidance was finalised 29 May 2026 (risk-based categories, new Decision Point D on whether validation data are needed; eSTAR asks for the HF category from 1 Aug 2026) **[snippet]**. The 864.3700 special control independently requires an HF engineering process; expect a formative/summative programme with 15+ pathologists on critical tasks (slide/case identity, magnification awareness, annotation persistence, Dx-mode indicators).

**Labelling, UDI, records.** Stand-alone software displays its UDI in plain text at start-up or via an "About" menu; software not distributed in packaged form complies if the production identifier carries the version **[snippet]**; GUDID entry per device identifier. Part 11 applies to the electronic records we keep for the QMS and to clinical-study data (2003 scope guidance; 2023 draft on electronic systems in clinical investigations) **[unverified]**.

### F5. Clinical and analytical validation for a viewer Dx claim

**Designs used by scanner predicates [verified, PubMed]:** multicentre, enriched, randomised, blinded crossover reader studies against the original sign-out as reference; adjudication panel classifies major/minor discordance; non-inferiority of the WSI-vs-glass major-discordance difference with a 4 % margin (upper bound of 95 % CI). Philips 1,992 cases/16 readers/4 sites, washout ≥4 weeks; Leica AT2 DX 2,045 cases/19 readers/5 sites, washout ≥31 days; Hamamatsu 2,000 cases/4 sites/4 readers per site, Mar 2019–Jul 2021 (28 months); Leica GT 450 DX 1,161 cases/10 readers/3 sites incl. remote viewing; Roche 2,047 cases plus a precision study (23 features; 89–90 % agreement, LB ≥85 %).

**What a software-only viewer must show.** Pixel-pathway equivalence to the predicate on the named scanner formats and display: colour reproducibility (ICC handling, test slides), spatial resolution/no information loss across pyramid levels and resampling, navigation/zoom behaviour and time-to-render, annotation/measurement accuracy, slide/case identity integrity, display QA requirements in labelling; cybersecurity and HF files. PathPresenter did this with no reader study **[snippet]**. A compact confirmatory reader study (200–400 cases, 3–4 pathologists, 2-week washout, adjudication) is cheap insurance if FDA asks, and doubles as the CAP validation for the first UT lab.

**Cost and duration [model, inputs stated].** Pivotal scanner-class study: ~16,000 reads × 5 min ≈ 1,330 pathologist-hours of reading plus ~1,000 adjudication hours, 2,000-case curation/scanning, CRO/data management, statistics, site fees: $2–5 M and 18–30 months. Viewer confirmatory study (300 cases × 2 modalities × 4 readers = 2,400 reads ≈ 200 h; adjudication 100 h; curation 300 h): $150–400 k, 6–9 months, far less inside an academic department that supplies its own pathologists. CAP-style validation (60 cases per application, 1–4 pathologists): 10–20 pathologist-hours each, $10–25 k opportunity cost per application. Bench TPA programme (colour targets, resolution phantoms, display photometry, latency rig): $50–150 k plus instruments.

### F6. Who holds the clearance: legal models and precedents

**Who may be the manufacturer.** Any legal entity that "initiates specifications" for a device made by others, or makes it itself, is a manufacturer/specification developer under 21 CFR 807.3 and 820.3 and must register, list, pay the establishment fee unless exempt, and run the QMS **[snippet/unverified]**. **Texas-specific law.**
- **Tort Claims Act** §101.023(a): state liability capped at $250,000 per person, $500,000 per occurrence, $100,000 property **[snippet, statute text]**; immunity is waived only for use of tangible personal property or motor vehicles; *UTMB v. York* held information is not tangible personal property and *Sawyer* applied that to computer output **[snippet]**. A UT-held viewer would likely be shielded from software-defect tort claims in Texas courts, though out-of-state claims and federal claims are different questions **[unverified]**.
- **No indemnities.** Tex. Const. art. III §49 and AG opinion MW-475: a state agency "will ordinarily be unable to execute an enforceable indemnity"; UT Tyler and TTUHSC contracting offices strike indemnity clauses **[snippet]**. Hospitals that receive the device will therefore get no indemnity from a UT holder; they will ask for insurance or a nonprofit counterparty.
- **Charitable Immunity and Liability Act** (ch. 84): caps of $500 k/person, $1 M/occurrence, $100 k property for charities and their employees, conditional on carrying that much insurance (a $1 M combined single limit suffices) **[snippet]**.
- **IP.** Regents' Rule 90101: the Board owns software created by employees; creators cannot license it themselves; UT Austin and UT Southwestern have procedures that permit open-source release (GPL "or other similar" licences) when in the university's and public interest **[snippet]**. An Apache-2.0 release needs the campus technology-commercialisation office to approve the licence choice.

**Fees for a government or nonprofit holder.** 21 U.S.C. 379j(a)(2)(B)(iii): no 510(k)/De Novo/PMA fee for a submission "by a State or Federal Government entity unless the device involved is to be distributed commercially"; the same section exempts the establishment fee for establishments operated by a State or Federal entity or tribe, same proviso **[snippet, statute text]**. Small-business status (≤$100 M gross receipts on the federal income tax return, Form 3602N, guidance finalised 31 Jul 2025) is written for tax filers; how FDA treats a 501(c)(3) or a university that files no income-tax return could not be determined **[open]**. Registration-fee hardship waivers exist only for renewals and ≤$1 M receipts **[snippet]**.

**Post-market obligations and their cost [model; obligations unverified in detail].** Annual registration/listing ($13,785 unless exempt); GUDID; complaint files; MDR under Part 803 (30-day reports for deaths, serious injuries, reportable malfunctions; eMDR); corrections and removals under Part 806 (a software patch that reduces a risk to health is a reportable correction; routine cybersecurity patches are not **[unverified]**); CAPA, internal audit, management review; §524B post-market vulnerability monitoring and disclosure; labelling and UDI maintenance per release. With a lean team (0.5 FTE quality, 0.5 FTE regulatory, 0.5 FTE security, shared engineering) this is $350–700 k/year including tooling and the registration fee; it does not scale much with the number of hospitals, but complaint volume and support do.

**Insurance.** Start-up medians are ~$3.7 k/year for tech E&O and ~$2.9 k for cyber, but hospital contracts commonly demand $5–10 M Tech E&O/cyber limits and many digital-health policies exclude bodily injury **[snippet]**. A Class II SaMD distributed to many hospitals should budget $25–75 k/year for product liability + E&O + cyber at $5 M limits **[unverified]**; a UT holder may self-insure through the System.

**International later.** EU IVDR Class C with notified body and UKCA (note 09); the EU Cyber Resilience Act reaches even the RUO build from Sept 2026/Dec 2027 (note 09). Not before US clearance.

### F7. Open source and the controlled, cleared release

- **Licence.** Apache-2.0 for the core and SDKs (patent grant; note 09), DCO for contributions, trademark policy; MPL-2.0 is the only copyleft alternative compatible with hospital procurement; AGPL is not. Tempus's consortium licence is unannounced; aligning with it (or being able to consume it) argues against anything stronger than Apache/MPL **[analysis]**.
- **"Anyone may modify" vs "the cleared configuration is the one we signed".** Clearance attaches to the holder's device: a specific build, SBOM, configuration manifest and labelling. Technical enforcement: reproducible builds, Sigstore-signed artefacts and containers, a configuration hash baked into the Dx-mode gate, an "About" screen with UDI/version/hash, attestation reported to the holder's telemetry (PHI-free) when a Dx-mode session starts on a non-signed build. Legal enforcement: the distribution agreement forbids modifying the Dx build for clinical use and reserves the trademark for signed builds; a hospital that modifies and uses the software clinically becomes a manufacturer of its own device (as OsiriX/Horos users are warned) **[snippet/analysis]**.
- **Governance.** A steering committee (clinical, engineering, security, regulatory, two member hospitals), a public roadmap, a public QMS (Tidepool's qmsOS and the ELISA write-up are the template) **[snippet]**, security.txt and CVD policy, and a published "cleared configuration" register per release.

## 3. Implications for our viewer and programme

1. **The Dx edition is a QKQ 510(k) with two scanner pairings and one or two displays, plus a PCCP.** Pick S360MD (NDPI) and GT 450 DX (SVS/DICOM) because every predicate used them; pick Barco MDPC-8127 and one Dell medical display because they appear in cleared pairings. Engineering must freeze and version the pixel pathway (codec, resampling, ICC, Dx-mode gate, rendering-complete state) before verification starts (note 09).
2. **Two codebases in one repo, segregated per IEC 62304 §4.3.** Class C pixel pathway and case/slide identity; Class A/B everything else (education, collaboration, research adapters). Segregation has to be an architectural fact (process/module boundary, no shared mutable state) that a reviewer can see.
3. **Design controls from the first commit.** Requirements with IDs, traceability to tests, golden-image tests per browser/OS, SBOM in CI, signed builds, risk file. Reconstructing these later costs more than the clearance.
4. **The RUO and education products need different names, builds and claims**, not a flag, or the RUO guidance's totality test bites.
5. **Ask FDA early.** A Pre-Sub (free, 70-day written feedback **[snippet]**) should carry five questions: QKQ with viewer predicate acceptable; documentation level; reader study needed for a viewer with these pairings; PCCP scope for scanners/formats/displays; whether free, non-commercial distribution by a State entity is "commercial distribution" for 807.81 and for the 379j fee exemption.
6. **Build CAP-validation tooling into the product** (note 11-gap-7); it becomes our confirmatory reader study instrument and the hospitals' qualification tool.
